# Rhode Island Government Guide and Site Ideas
## Build capsule for Rhode Island Hurricane

### Working direction

Build a Rhode Island civic-service and government-research section using the existing Rhode Island Hurricane publication as the first home. Keep it distinct from the site's events and general news sections. If the section earns a regular audience and can be maintained, it can later become a standalone sister publication.

### Purpose

Help Rhode Islanders:

1. Understand what state and local government is discussing, proposing, deciding, and funding.
2. Identify which office is responsible for a problem and how to reach a real person.
3. Follow projects and proposals from announcement through final outcome.
4. See what a public decision or project means for their town and daily life.

### Product sections

- **Get help:** Start with the resident's situation and town; identify the responsible office, the best verified contact route, what to have ready, suggested questions, and a next step.
- **Government activity:** Plain-language summaries of proposals, public meetings, decisions, regulations, and major announcements.
- **Highway and infrastructure tracker:** Projects by town and route, with purpose, phase, schedule, traffic effects, cost changes, funding sources, responsible agency, and the next milestone.
- **Budget and funding:** Follow a funding item from proposal to adopted budget to later spending reports. Label proposed, enacted, revised, obligated, and spent amounts separately.
- **Status history:** Preserve dated changes so readers can see when a plan advanced, changed, paused, or ended.

### Status and sourcing rules

Use official records as the basis for facts. Cite the record title and publication or meeting date, and record when the item was last checked.

Use explicit statuses such as proposed, under review, in committee, approved, funded, contracted, under construction, completed, withdrawn, or cancelled only when the source supports that label. Use “no recent action found” when the record does not establish that a proposal was shelved. Do not infer that approved money has been spent, or that a proposed project is funded.

Keep reporting neutral. Separate what a document says from the publication's plain-language explanation. Publish corrections and retain the earlier status history. Name individual staff only when their public role and contact details are verified.

### First pilot

Follow one well-documented Rhode Island highway project as a test case:

- Establish the project's official purpose, location, current phase, schedule, and stated funding.
- Collect dated agency updates and public records.
- Explain what changed, when, what residents will experience, and what happens next.
- Link the project record to the responsible public office and the best verified contact path.
- Keep planned schedule, revised estimate, approved budget, and reported spending distinct.

RIDOT's quarterly accountability reports and project-by-municipality materials are candidate source sets. State budget volumes and capital-budget documents are candidate sources for the funding layer. Verify each record before publication.

### Publication network

- **Rhode Island Government Guide:** Deeper research, status timelines, budget explanations, and verified ways to reach the responsible office.
- **Rhode Island Hurricane:** Concise statewide updates when a government story has broad public value.
- **Jamestown Island News:** Local reporting on decisions, projects, and funding that affect Jamestown.

Build one verified research record, then write each publication's version for its own audience. Do not copy the same full story across all three.

### Prototype and launch sequence

1. Add a separate government section to Rhode Island Hurricane, leaving the current events and general-news structure intact.
2. Start with the help navigator and one highway-project tracker.
3. Add proposal and budget timelines after the source-verification workflow is reliable.
4. Measure whether readers return, use contact information, and follow tracked projects.
5. Consider a standalone sister publication only after the section has a repeat audience and a sustainable update process.

### Other Rhode Island site concepts

Ranked qualitatively by likely repeat use and breadth of audience; this is not measured search-volume data.

1. **Rhode Island Family Daybook:** School calendars, closure and bus alerts, early dismissals, family programs, and school-year deadlines.
2. **Rhode Island Commute and Roadwork:** Planned construction, bridge work, detours, lane closures, and route impacts.
3. **Beach, Water, and Outdoor Conditions:** Beach closures, water advisories, parking, park notices, fishing and shellfish alerts.
4. **Housing and Renter Navigator:** Plain-language housing help, programs, eligibility, openings, and verified contacts.
5. **Childcare and After-School Finder:** Provider filters by town, child age, schedule, affordability, and confirmed availability.
6. **Town Services Finder:** Recycling, transfer stations, parking bans, permits, office hours, and public notices.
7. **Jobs and Training Guide:** Current local openings, apprenticeships, workforce training, licensing steps, and job fairs.
8. **Small-Business Start and Permit Guide:** Registration, licenses, inspections, taxes, hiring, and funding steps.
9. **Senior and Caregiver Services Guide:** Meals, transportation, benefits, home support, and local services.

### Evaluation before expansion

For each concept, record the target audience, recurring problem, essential data sources, update frequency, maintenance effort, and a simple test of whether people would use it again. Prioritize concepts with frequent needs, broad Rhode Island relevance, dependable sources, and manageable upkeep.
