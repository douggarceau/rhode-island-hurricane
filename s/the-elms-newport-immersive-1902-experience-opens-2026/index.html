The Elms in Newport Opens a Multisensory Trip Back to the Summer of 1902

Newport Mansions and design studio Digital Kitchen spent three years building an immersive tour with sounds, scents and interactive displays.

The Elms, the Gilded Age mansion on Bellevue Avenue in Newport, has opened a multisensory museum experience that takes visitors back to the summer of 1902, according to NBC 10 News. The project, three years in the making, uses sights, sounds, smells and interactive displays, and NBC 10 described it as a first for Rhode Island and the country.

What happened

Newport Mansions developed the experience with Digital Kitchen, an Emmy Award-winning design studio, NBC 10 reported Monday. Visitors tour the home of Edward Berwind and his wife, Herminie, hearing a voice representing Edward Berwind, watching scenes of servants at work and stepping into the mansion's kitchen, where they encounter some of the house's more unusual guests. An immersive ballroom segment is designed to make visitors feel they have stepped back in time.

Along the way, the tour explains who designed and decorated the house, who the Berwinds were, the well-known guests they entertained and the staff who ran the estate behind the scenes.

Why the mansion is trying something new

Melanie Garcia, director of visitor experience, told NBC 10 that the team wanted to draw people who might not otherwise visit a traditional historic house, including younger and more diverse audiences. She described history as something everyone should learn about and called the new experience a fun way to do it. She said there are early conversations about expanding the use of technology.

When the partnership with Digital Kitchen was first announced, the Newport Daily News reported that the Preservation Society of Newport County, which operates Newport Mansions, wanted to improve the visitor experience and keep its standing among the nation's top cultural attractions. The organization draws nearly 800,000 visits a year, the paper reported. Chief Executive Trudy Coxe said then that the group must keep embracing new ideas, and Digital Kitchen's head of experiences said the studio hoped to help visitors rediscover the mansions' history. The Elms was chosen as the first property to receive the new treatment.

Garcia told NBC 10 that the house, completed in 1901, was ahead of its time and built around technology. The mansion has also served as a filming location for HBO's series The Gilded Age. Garcia noted that its kitchens resemble those seen on the show, and that viewers may recognize the ballroom as the Russell family's ballroom.

What it means for visitors

For Rhode Islanders and tourists, the change turns a self-guided walk through period rooms into a staged, story-driven visit. The approach is aimed at people who enjoy history on screen as much as in museums, and it arrives as the holiday season brings heavy traffic to Bellevue Avenue's mansions. NBC 10's report did not list ticket prices or hours for the new experience; the station directed readers to the project's website, theelmsexperience.com, for details.

What comes next

Newport Mansions has said The Elms is the first of its properties to get an interactive storytelling experience from Digital Kitchen, and Garcia said conversations about expanding the technology are at an early stage. No timetable for similar projects at other mansions was reported.
