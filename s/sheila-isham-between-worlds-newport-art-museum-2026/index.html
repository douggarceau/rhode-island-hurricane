Sheila Isham: Between Worlds at the Newport Art Museum Through Jan. 4

Open Monday and Wednesday through Sunday, 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., at the Newport Art Museum, 76 Bellevue Avenue, Newport, with the exhibition running through Jan. 4, 2027.

Sheila Isham spent more than six decades making paintings, and the Newport Art Museum's current presentation, Sheila Isham: Between Worlds, gathers more than thirty of her works from 1968 to 2004. The show is organized in chapters that trace her life and career, and it stays on view through January 4, 2027.

Her abstractions grew out of the Washington Color School, the group of painters associated with Sam Gilliam and Kenneth Noland, whose work relied on broad fields of color and on the way paint behaves on a large canvas. Isham's own life was more far-flung. She lived in Berlin, Moscow, Paris, Hong Kong, Washington and New Delhi, and through her marriage to Heyward Isham she spent summers in Newport, which is how the island entered her story.

The work

Most of the pieces are large-scale acrylics on canvas. Titles in the exhibition include Sun – Penetrating Wind (1968), Energy Fields Series, Lü – Fire with Mountain (1971), Phoenix Rising (1972) and two paintings from the Cosmic Myth Series, dated 1980 and 1980–1981. Isham drew on Taoist philosophy and Jungian psychology, and she saw painting as a spiritual discipline, an idea that comes through in the cosmic and elemental titles. Her work is held by the Smithsonian American Art Museum, the National Gallery of Art and the Baltimore Museum of Art.

Visiting

The museum is at 76 Bellevue Avenue in Newport and is open Monday and Wednesday through Sunday from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. It is closed on Tuesdays and on several holidays, including Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. Admission is $20 for adults, $15 for visitors 65 and older and $10 for college students. Members, active military with a spouse, and visitors 17 and under pay no charge. The phone number is (401) 848-8200. A 71-page catalogue by Patricia L. Lewy, director of the Sheila Isham Estate Collection and Archives, is sold at the museum for $30.

The museum's fall calendar also includes a talk called Experiencing the Abstract: Ways of Seeing on November 18, a natural companion to a show built around abstract painting.
