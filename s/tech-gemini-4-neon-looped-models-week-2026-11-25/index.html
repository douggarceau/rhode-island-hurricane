Gemini 4 Neon Undercuts Its Big Brother as AI Models Start Looping

Google's new mid-tier model nearly matches its flagship for half the price, and three labs bet on a stranger way to build AI.

What happened

Google put Gemini 4 Neon on sale on Nov. 18, slotting a new middle child into its Gemini 4 family between the top-end Carbon and the lighter Hydrogen. According to the release tracker Capital & Compute, Neon is generally available on Google's paid Gemini API, in AI Studio, in the Antigravity coding tool and in the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers.

It did not arrive alone. The NoCode MBA release tracker, updated Nov. 24, lists well over a dozen model launches between Nov. 17 and Nov. 24. The pattern worth noticing sits at the edges of that list: three different labs shipped models built on the same unusual design, a so-called loop transformer.

Neon in plain English

Neon is pitched at complicated software work, office knowledge work and science, per NoCode MBA's summary of Google's announcement. It can hold about 1 million tokens of context at once, which in practice means a large codebase or a stack of long documents in a single conversation.

Capital & Compute reports that Neon was adapted from Carbon, Google's biggest Gemini 4 model. The pitch is simple: most of Carbon's smarts at a lower price per job.

The loop trick, explained

A standard large language model passes each word through a long stack of distinct layers, one after another. A looped model reuses some of those layers, running the same block several times on each word. The idea is to get more thinking out of fewer stored parts.

Three examples landed within a week, according to NoCode MBA. Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini on Nov. 20, an open-weight model whose middle layers run three times for every token, in a download of roughly 20 gigabytes. SenseTime released SenseNova-Loop-9B on Nov. 18, a model of about 9 billion parameters whose shared layers add up to 56 layer passes, with room for up to 512,000 tokens of context. And on Nov. 23 the Chinese lab Z.ai announced GLM-5.4, which it describes as the first flagship model built on a loop design, aimed at general coding.

Who is behind it

Neon comes from Google DeepMind, which has been filling out the Gemini 4 lineup all month. Capital & Compute and NoCode MBA both list Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Nov. 12, with Neon following six days later.

The loop models come from a wider cast: Thinking Machines Lab, a US startup; SenseTime, a veteran Chinese AI company; and Z.ai, which builds the GLM series of models. None of them is OpenAI, Google or Anthropic, which is part of the story. The new idea is coming from outside the usual top tier.

The numbers

Neon costs a flat $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at 15 cents, according to Capital & Compute. There is no introductory discount, and the model is not on Google's no-charge tier.

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a widely watched composite score, Capital & Compute lists Neon at 61 on its highest effort setting, at about $6.44 per task, and 60 on its default setting at about $3.74 per task. Carbon scores 60 at about $7.76 per task. Put another way, the cheaper model matches or edges the flagship on that index for about half the cost.

Carbon is also getting pricier. The tracker reports its rates rise to $4 input and $20 output per million tokens on Dec. 1.

What the rivals say

The competition was busy the same week. OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra, released Nov. 17, scores 62 on the same index, per Capital & Compute, one point above Neon. Its cheaper sibling GPT-6.2 Sol scores 57 at about $1.01 per task, which the tracker calls the cheapest route to any score in that range.

Microsoft also shipped on Nov. 17, according to NoCode MBA: MAI Code 2 and MAI Code 2 Flash for programmers inside GitHub Copilot, plus a security model and a real-time voice model.

A few caveats apply. Both trackers are third-party roundups, not the labs themselves, and Capital & Compute notes some of its cost figures are reported two different ways. Z.ai's GLM-5.4 is announced only, with no access date or price listed. And no one has yet shown that looped models beat conventional ones at the top end. The early examples are small or not yet public.

Why it matters to you

For most people, the headline is price. When a mid-tier model nearly matches a flagship for half the money, the apps built on top of it, from coding assistants to research tools, get cheaper to run. That tends to show up as better features in the versions people already pay for.

The loop experiments matter for a different reason. If reusing layers works at scale, capable models could get small enough to run on a laptop or a phone instead of in a distant data center. Inkling 2 Mini's roughly 20-gigabyte download is already in reach of a well-equipped home machine.

What comes next

Watch Dec. 1, when Carbon's price increase takes effect and developers decide whether Neon is good enough to switch. Watch for Z.ai to put a date and a price on GLM-5.4. And with Xpeng announcing a physical-AI model called WorldGPT on Nov. 24, per NoCode MBA, the next round of releases may be less about chatbots and more about machines that move.
