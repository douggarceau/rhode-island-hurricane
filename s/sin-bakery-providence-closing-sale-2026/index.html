Providence's Sin Bakery Closes After 24 Years With a Sale of Everything

The Westminster Street bakery is selling its supplies, furniture and decor as it closes after more than two decades downtown.

Sin Bakery, a fixture on Westminster Street in Providence for 24 years, is closing and holding an everything-must-go sale of its baking supplies, furniture and decorations, NBC 10 News reported. The sale runs on select days this month, with two more dates left later in November.

What is happening

Owner Jennifer Luxmoore is selling off the bakery's entire inventory. According to NBC 10, she hopes the sale will let people in the community carry a piece of the business forward. She told the station that the bakery lives on through the small pieces that customers take home.

The report did not give a specific reason for the closing.

Sale dates

The sale runs from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. on four days. The first two dates have already passed; the last two fall later this month.

How the bakery grew

According to NBC 10, Sin Bakery started out making custom cakes only. Over time it expanded into retail sales and plated desserts, becoming part of the restaurant and hospitality scene that has helped define downtown Providence.

Luxmoore told the station that being part of Rhode Island's hospitality industry has meant a great deal to her, and she praised the state's chefs and the wider community she worked alongside.

What it means for Providence

The closing removes a long-running independent business from Westminster Street, one of downtown Providence's main shopping and dining corridors. Small, owner-run food businesses have been a big part of the downtown mix for years, and the loss of one that lasted nearly a quarter century is a notable change for regular customers and neighbors.

For home bakers, cooks and other small businesses, the sale is a chance to pick up professional baking supplies and furnishings. For longtime customers, it is a last chance to visit the space.

What comes next

The final two sale days are Friday, Nov. 20, and Saturday, Nov. 21, from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. at the bakery on Westminster Street. NBC 10 did not report what will happen to the storefront after the bakery closes.
