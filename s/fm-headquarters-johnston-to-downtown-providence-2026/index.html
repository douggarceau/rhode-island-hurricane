FM to Move Global Headquarters From Johnston to Downtown Providence

The insurer is closing its 93-acre Johnston campus for a downtown tower, and Johnston's mayor says some workers have received layoff notices.

FM, the commercial property insurer long known as FM Global, is moving its global headquarters out of Johnston and into a downtown Providence office tower, closing the 93-acre corporate campus it has occupied on Central Avenue, according to reporting by Rhode Island Current. The company confirmed the plan in a statement on Tuesday, Nov. 10, and by Thursday Johnston Mayor Joseph Polisena Jr. was publicly criticizing the way the move was handled.

What happened

Rhode Island Current reported that FM confirmed the relocation on Tuesday afternoon. In its statement, the company said the move would bring its people closer together and strengthen its relationships with clients, partners and the investment community.

The new headquarters will be the 10-story office tower at 10 Memorial Boulevard in downtown Providence, the former IGT building. FM announced in February that it had bought the building, saying at the time that it planned to occupy part of the tower while keeping its existing mix of tenants. According to Rhode Island Current, the Providence building is expected to be ready in late 2027 after renovations.

The two sites are 5.4 miles apart, Rhode Island Current reported. The company has not publicly said how many employees will work in Providence, and the purchase price of the tower was not disclosed when FM announced the deal in February.

FM's Rhode Island roots run deep. The company says it was founded in 1835 and has been based in the state for nearly 200 years. It is a mutual insurer focused on property risk, and its policyholder-owners include about one in four Fortune 500 companies, according to the company.

What Johnston's mayor said

Polisena told Rhode Island Current on Thursday, Nov. 12, that FM is laying off an unknown number of employees as part of the relocation. He said he had met with an FM employee who told him that she and at least a handful of other workers had received layoff notices.

The mayor called the company's actions a "corporate sneak attack" and said the relocation news caught him by surprise. He also said that when his office called the company after his meeting with the employee, someone at FM hung up on his assistant. Rhode Island Current's report did not include a response from FM to the mayor's statements about layoffs or the phone call, and the number of affected jobs has not been confirmed by the company.

What state and city leaders said earlier

When FM announced the purchase of the Providence tower in February, Chief Operating Officer Randy Hodge said the investment showed the company's confidence in Rhode Island and its goal of attracting talent. Gov. Dan McKee said at the time that the investment showed Rhode Island is a strong place to do business, and Providence Mayor Brett Smiley described FM as a global leader and one of the state's largest employers. Senate President Valarie Lawson and House Speaker K. Joseph Shekarchi jointly called the purchase great news for the state.

FM's February announcement also noted that the company had leased new space in Boston's South Station Tower and planned to open a science and technology center in Norwood, Massachusetts, in 2026.

What it means for residents

For Johnston, the change means the departure of a longtime corporate headquarters and an open question about the future of a large property on Central Avenue. For downtown Providence, it means one of the state's largest employers, as Smiley described FM, moving its headquarters into a prominent office tower. Workers at the Johnston campus face uncertainty until the company says more about staffing.

What comes next

Renovation of the Providence tower is expected to continue into late 2027. FM has not publicly detailed what will happen to the Johnston campus once the headquarters moves, how many positions will shift to Providence, or how many, if any, will be eliminated. Residents and employees can expect more details as the company, the Town of Johnston and the City of Providence respond to the announcement in the coming weeks.
