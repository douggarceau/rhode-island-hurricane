Trinity Rep Marks 50 Years of A Christmas Carol

The Providence company's golden-anniversary production, directed by Taavon Gamble, plays Nov. 5 through Dec. 31 in the Dowling Theater.

For half a century Trinity Repertory Company has told Dickens's ghost story each winter, and this season the Providence troupe marks its 50th anniversary production of A Christmas Carol. It plays from November 5 through December 31, 2026.

A new director, a familiar tradition

The anniversary staging is directed by Taavon Gamble, a Trinity Rep company member who was also in last year's cast. Richard Cumming provides the original music that has long threaded through the production. The story follows Ebenezer Scrooge on his supernatural Christmas Eve journey through past, present and future, one of the most durable tales in the English-language theatre.

Last year's production, directed by Richard Jenkins and Sharon Jenkins, was a hit: most December performances sold out and the theatre added shows on December 30 and January 1. The Providence Journal's review called it "wonderfully magical and inspiring." That track record suggests early booking for this year's anniversary run.

Where to see it

Trinity Rep's Chace Theater is being renovated, so the entire 2026-27 season is being staged downstairs in the company's Dowling Theater. Productions in the more intimate room tend to put audiences close to the performers, which suits a story built on ghosts, memory and the small kindnesses of a family table.

The annual production has traditionally been a gathering place for Rhode Island families, and Trinity Rep has in past years invited local children to take part in the cast; the 2025 production included eight children chosen from open auditions.

Tickets

Performance times and ticket prices were not published on the pages consulted. The full 2026-27 schedule and ticketing are available through Trinity Rep at trinityrep.com. Given the run's popularity in recent years, December dates are likely to go quickly.

The season continues in January with Cyrano and, in February, Mother Russia, both in rotating repertory, but for the holidays it is hard to beat a fifty-year tradition.
