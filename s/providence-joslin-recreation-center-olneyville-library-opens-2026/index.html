Providence Opens Renovated Joslin Rec Center and New Olneyville Library

A $5.5 million project in Olneyville combines a renovated recreation center, a new library branch and a climate resilience hub.

Providence city leaders on Monday celebrated the completion of a $5.5 million project at the Joslin Recreation Center in Olneyville that renovated the center, opened the new Olneyville Library and reopened the neighborhood's community resilience hub at 17 Hyatt St., according to NBC 10 News. The work was paid for with a mix of federal American Rescue Plan Act money, city funds and state funds.

What happened

Mayor Brett Smiley cut the ribbon Monday morning alongside Mayor-elect David Morales, U.S. Rep. Gabe Amo, City Councilman Oscar Vargas and other city leaders, NBC 10 reported. Smiley described the building as a hub where families can meet their needs during the school day, after school and in the summer, and said there is now something for everyone there. City leaders called the project a win for Providence, with the greatest benefit going to Olneyville, the station reported.

What is inside

According to a city project page, the renovation covers both the recreation center and the Olneyville Resilience Hub, which occupies the building's second floor. The city said floor plans were based on the programs and services that residents asked for during a series of community meetings, and design renderings showed a cyber lobby, multipurpose rooms and an education room.

The resilience hub first opened in January 2024, the city said. It is meant to help residents prepare for environmental hazards such as flooding and extreme heat or cold, and it offers emergency preparedness and climate resilience trainings along with regular community programs. The project also brings the Olneyville Library into the building. NBC 10's report did not give the library's hours or details about its collection.

How it was paid for

The city's project page says the mayor's administration secured $3.3 million in state funding for the building renovations, and that the city planned to seek additional money with community organizations for parts of the project the grant could not cover. NBC 10 reported the finished $5.5 million project drew on American Rescue Plan Act dollars, city money and state money. The station's report did not break down how much came from each source beyond the state figure listed by the city.

What it means for residents

For Olneyville families, the project puts recreation programs, library services and emergency-preparedness resources under one roof in the middle of the neighborhood. The resilience hub is designed to serve as a gathering point when severe weather or other emergencies strike, while the library and recreation spaces serve students and adults on ordinary days. City officials have said the design was shaped by neighbors' own requests for programs and services.

What comes next

The ribbon cutting comes weeks before a change in leadership at City Hall, with Mayor-elect Morales attending alongside the outgoing mayor. Residents can find updates on programs at the center through the city's recreation department and its sustainability office, which oversees the resilience hub. The city has not announced a schedule of new programs at the renovated building in the reports reviewed.
