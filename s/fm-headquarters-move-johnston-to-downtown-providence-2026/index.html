FM Will Move Its Global Headquarters From Johnston to Downtown Providence

The insurer founded in Providence in 1835 will return to the city at 10 Memorial Boulevard, with renovations due in late 2027.

FM, the commercial property insurer founded in Providence in 1835, plans to move its global headquarters from its campus in Johnston to downtown Providence, the company confirmed on Wednesday, according to Business Insurance and The Boston Globe. The insurer will occupy part of 10 Memorial Boulevard, a 10-story office building it bought in February, with renovations expected to wrap up in late 2027.

What happened

FM confirmed the plan on Nov. 11, Business Insurance reported. The company said the move is part of a wider strategy to grow its presence in major Northeast business centers along the Interstate 95 and Amtrak corridors. The trade publication noted that the move returns the company to the city where it was founded nearly two centuries ago.

FM bought 10 Memorial Boulevard for $68.5 million in February, according to published reports cited by Business Insurance. At the time, the company said in a news release that it would occupy part of the building and keep it as a multi-tenant office property.

What happens to Johnston

An FM spokesperson told Business Insurance that the Johnston campus will eventually stop serving as an FM work location, but said no decision has been made on when that will happen. The company is still evaluating plans for the property, the spokesperson said. FM had not answered questions about possible job changes or how many employees work at the Johnston headquarters, according to the publication.

FM's research campus in West Glocester will remain open, Business Insurance reported. The company also leases office space in Boston's South Station Tower and announced in February that it would open a science and technology center in Norwood, Massachusetts.

Who said what

When FM announced the building purchase in February, Chief Operating Officer Randy Hodge said the investment reflects FM's confidence in Rhode Island, according to the company's news release.

Gov. Dan McKee said in the same release that the state's story cannot be told without FM. Providence Mayor Brett P. Smiley called FM a global leader and one of the state's largest employers. Senate President Valarie J. Lawson and House Speaker K. Joseph Shekarchi jointly described the company as a deeply valued neighbor and employer and welcomed its growth in Rhode Island.

What it means for residents

For downtown Providence, the move brings one of the state's largest employers into the city's core, a district where businesses and city leaders have long sought more daily office workers. For Johnston, the decision raises questions about the future of a large commercial campus and the property tax revenue tied to it, though the company has said no timeline has been set.

Commuters may also notice changes over time. FM has framed the move around access to rail and highway connections, and 10 Memorial Boulevard sits near Providence Station and Kennedy Plaza, the city's main bus hub.

What comes next

Renovations at 10 Memorial Boulevard are expected to be finished in late 2027, Business Insurance reported. FM has not said when employees will move or what will become of the Johnston campus. Town and state officials have not announced any plans for the site.
