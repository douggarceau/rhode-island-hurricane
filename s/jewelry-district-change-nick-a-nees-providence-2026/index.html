A 30-Year-Old Bar Tests How Much the Jewelry District Has Changed

Rising rents in Providence's fast-growing Jewelry District have put the long-running Nick-A-Nees music bar on South Street in a tight spot.

Nick-A-Nees, a music bar that has operated at 75 South Street in Providence's Jewelry District since 1996, is negotiating a new lease after a sharp rent increase, according to a report published Friday by The Providence Eye. The bar's situation highlights how much the neighborhood has changed as Brown University, life sciences firms and new housing have filled the area once dominated by jewelry factories.

What happened

The bar's rent rose by $1,800 a month in September 2025 and now stands at just under $7,000 a month, the Eye reported. The owner told the publication that the combined cost of rent, food and liquor, insurance, payroll and licensing has become difficult to cover.

In August, staff started an online fundraiser that more than doubled its goal, according to the report, with the money going toward overdue rent, the liquor license and other bills. The owner is now negotiating with the property owner and told the Eye she is seeking a 10-year lease that would let the bar keep its character, and that she is prepared to walk away if that is not possible.

How the neighborhood got here

In 1880, Rhode Island produced more than a quarter of the nation's jewelry, much of it made in about 11 blocks west of the Providence River, the Eye noted, citing the district's National Register of Historic Places application. Interstate 195 split the neighborhood in the 1950s, urban renewal cleared much of its housing, and by the 1970s the jewelry industry was leaving. Old factory buildings were later converted to offices, medical space and condominiums.

When the bar opened, few businesses wanted to locate there, and the city granted it an economic development liquor license under then-Mayor Vincent Cianci, according to the report.

A second campus for Brown

The biggest change has come from Brown University. A developer who had owned 222 Richmond Street since 1977 sold it and six other properties to Brown, in what the Eye described as one of the university's largest acquisitions. That building now houses the Warren Alpert Medical School. Brown has since bought about 10 parcels in the area, turned a former power plant into offices at South Street Landing, and is building what the Eye described as the state's largest academic laboratory building. Development on former I-195 land has added more construction nearby.

Brown President Christina Paxson said in 2015 that the university aimed to be a partner in building a mix of academic, commercial and innovation activity in the district, the Eye noted.

Who said what

Sharon Steele, president of the Jewelry District Association, told the Eye the neighborhood is in a constant state of upheaval from construction. She said the district has more construction and new housing than almost anywhere in the city, that its future centers on biomedicine and life sciences, and that roughly 400 new workers could soon arrive on a single block.

The bar's owner said rising property values and taxes in the area are behind the higher rent, according to the report.

What it means and what comes next

For Providence, the Jewelry District has become one of the city's main economic growth areas, bringing jobs, research money and new tax base. The same growth is raising costs for older, smaller businesses that arrived when the area was largely empty. No city action is pending on the bar's situation; its future depends on the outcome of the lease talks, which were continuing as of this week.
