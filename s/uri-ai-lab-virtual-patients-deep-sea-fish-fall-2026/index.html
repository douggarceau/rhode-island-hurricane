Virtual Patients and Deep-Sea Fish Spotters: Inside URI's AI Lab

The University of Rhode Island's AI lab says it is building virtual patients for pharmacy students, sonar-and-camera iceberg models and AI clinics for small businesses.

At the University of Rhode Island in Kingston, artificial intelligence is being put to work on everything from training pharmacists to finding fish hundreds of meters below the surface. A fall 2026 update from URI's Institute for AI and Computational Research lays out more than a dozen projects that its AI lab has taken on with partners across campus, and several are headed for expansion.

Who is behind it

The work comes from the AI lab inside URI's Institute for AI and Computational Research. The lab reports serving 45 students in spring 2026, 27 over the summer and 45 this fall, pairing them with faculty partners. The update also notes that Gabriele Fariello, Gaurav Khanna and Indrani Mandal were invited by the Rhode Island Senate Committee on Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies to present on AI at the State House.

Virtual patients for future pharmacists

The flagship health project is the AI Clinical Learning Environment, or AICLE, built with Dr. Todd Brothers of the College of Pharmacy. It uses AI-generated virtual patients so students can practice medication decisions, with faculty reviewing the cases. The lab says it is designed to protect patient privacy, and that the longer-term goal is to expand it to nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, medicine and physician assistant education.

From the deep sea to the Arctic

URI's ocean focus shows up across the list. OceanDetect, led by Dr. Andy Davies, uses machine learning to detect and label fish in underwater video from 200 to 800 meters deep. A project with Dr. Russell Shomberg of Ocean Engineering and the Graduate School of Oceanography reconstructed Arctic sea ice in 3D and was published in the OCEANS 2026 Monterey proceedings; the next step is a system that fuses multibeam sonar and cameras to model an iceberg's keel. Another team, with Prof. Ying Zhang, plans to use AI models to look for proteins that can break down plastic.

AI for campus and for Main Street

Some tools are aimed at the university itself. A Faculty Senate assistant uses large language models running on URI's Harmony high-performance computing system to review course and program proposals in two passes, with a cheaper model checking course numbers and a stronger one checking formatting. An engineering chatbot built with Prof. Resit Sendag answers graduate program questions and is slated to expand to other departments.

Off campus, the update points to small businesses. It cites a $10 million Google.org initiative for America's Small Business Development Centers to build AI curriculum and clinics for 100,000 small businesses nationwide, and says two AI lab students will support student-led projects. The lab is also running AI workshops for rural healthcare providers, supported in part by the federal Rural Health Transformation Program.

Why it matters

Rhode Island has been pushing to be a serious player in AI, and URI's lab shows what that looks like at ground level: students building working tools for pharmacists, oceanographers, administrators and business owners rather than just studying the technology. The update also mentions a summer AI and quantum computing camp for Rhode Island high school students and an adult-learning course on using AI safely.

What comes next

According to the update, coming work includes expanding AICLE to other health programs, continuing the rural healthcare workshops, finishing the iceberg keel reconstruction, widening the chatbot and launching the small-business AI clinics. The full report is posted on the institute's website. Its figures are the lab's own and were not independently verified for this story.
