Kingston Station Bridge Replacement Begins Tonight, Changing Route 138 Access

RIDOT shifts Route 138 traffic in South Kingstown tonight as a two-stage rebuild of the bridge over the rail line gets underway, with completion set for 2028.

The Rhode Island Department of Transportation is scheduled to begin replacing the Kingston Station Railroad Bridge in South Kingstown tonight, Friday, Nov. 20, setting up traffic changes on Route 138 that are expected to last about a year for the first phase alone. The bridge carries Route 138, also known as Kingstown Road, over the railroad tracks beside the station served by Amtrak trains and RIPTA buses.

What Happened

In a travel advisory issued Thursday, RIDOT said it will make a series of temporary traffic pattern changes on Route 138 at Railroad Avenue, the access road to Kingston Station. All traffic on the bridge will be moved to its north side while crews demolish and rebuild the southern half. The department said two-way traffic will be kept at all times.

After about a year, traffic will shift onto the new southern span so the northern half can be torn down and replaced. RIDOT said the full replacement is expected to be finished by the end of 2028.

Changes for Drivers and Riders

RIDOT listed several changes taking effect with the work:

A temporary traffic signal and crosswalks will be installed at Route 138 and Railroad Avenue. All access to the station will be through the southern Railroad Avenue entrance, where a traffic island will be removed so drivers from both directions on Route 138 can use it.

Part of Railroad Avenue where it passes under Route 138 will close, and 10 spaces will be lost in the northern overflow lot. People parking there will need to use the new Route 138 crosswalk and walk along the Railroad Avenue sidewalks.

The dedicated left-turn lane for westbound Route 138 at Fairgrounds Road will be removed until the bridge work is done. South County Bike Path users may still park in the main lot but must use spaces near the trailhead at the far end, and University of Rhode Island parking is not allowed at the station.

RIDOT said it timed the start of the work to follow a parking expansion completed last summer, which added 129 spaces at the station for a total of 344.

What It Means for Residents

Commuters who use Kingston Station, URI-area drivers and people heading to the bike path should expect a new traffic signal, one station entrance instead of two and some extra walking from the overflow lot. The advisory did not describe any change to Amtrak or RIPTA service.

What Comes Next

The bridge work is one piece of a larger $82.3 million Route 138 corridor project that will add sidewalks, upgrade signals and repave Route 138 from Route 2 to Route 108. RIDOT said that project is still in design, with construction hoped to begin later in 2027 and finish in the spring of 2029. A map of station access changes is posted at ridot.net/DetourMaps, and the department said all schedules may change because of weather and other conditions.
