Brown Awards $4.5 Million to Train Rhode Island Health Care and Factory Workers

Four Rhode Island nonprofits and institutions will expand training for nursing aides, medical assistants, machinists and early childhood clinicians.

Brown University announced Wednesday that it is awarding $4.5 million in workforce development grants to four Rhode Island organizations, money meant to train hundreds of residents for jobs in health care, manufacturing and early childhood mental health. It is the second round of spending under a $50 million commitment the university made to the state's workforce as part of its July 2025 agreement with the federal government.

What Happened

According to Brown's announcement, three organizations received anchor grants of up to $1.5 million each and a fourth received a smaller innovation grant. The recipients are the Genesis Center in Providence, the manufacturing partnership Polaris MEP, the University of Rhode Island Foundation, and the Rhode Island Association for Infant Mental Health.

The Boston Globe reported that the grants follow the voluntary resolution agreement Brown reached with the federal government, which restored about $510 million in federal research funding. The Globe described the agreement as controversial and noted that Brown did not admit wrongdoing as part of it.

The Genesis Center, which says it has trained more than 1,000 frontline health care workers over the past decade, will expand three existing programs and add a fourth. Students can earn credentials as certified nursing assistants, medical assistants, dental assistants and medical interpreters. Brown University Health, Providence Community Health Centers and the Comprehensive Community Action Program are named partners.

Polaris MEP will work with JARC Rhode Island, the New England Institute of Technology and the Community College of Rhode Island on group training in CNC machining, welding and industrial maintenance. The URI College of Pharmacy, which now trains about 18 nursing assistants a year to become medication aides, will be able to train up to 90 a year for nursing homes, assisted living centers and rehabilitation programs. The infant mental health group will create what Brown called the state's first workforce hub for early childhood mental health, serving children from birth to age five.

Who Said What

Mary Jo Callan, Brown's vice president for community engagement, said the grants are building a stronger economy by creating real opportunities for Rhode Islanders. She said the programs go beyond classroom training by offering support services and direct connections to employers, and that all of the grants target worker shortages.

Shannon Carroll, president and CEO of the Genesis Center, thanked the university and said a good job can change the course of a person's life.

What It Means for Residents

Together with a $3 million first round in January to the Community College of Rhode Island and Building Futures, Brown has now committed about $7.5 million. The university says the two rounds will open training opportunities for more than 1,200 Rhode Islanders, many in fields where employers report shortages.

What Comes Next

Brown said the next funding cycle is expected to open in the spring of 2027. Proposals are reviewed by a committee of Brown faculty, staff and students along with Providence community and business leaders, who weigh labor market demand, program design and employer hiring commitments. About $42.5 million of the $50 million pledge remains to be awarded.
