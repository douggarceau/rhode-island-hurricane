Fiber Internet Goes Live for First Newport Customers With $9 Million in State Aid

GoNetspeed has connected its first Newport homes and businesses to a fiber network backed by federal money routed through Rhode Island Commerce.

Newport's first customers are now connected to a new fiber-optic internet network, GoNetspeed announced on Tuesday, Nov. 17, marking the start of service in a citywide buildout supported by $9 million in public funds, What's Up Newp reported.

What happened

GoNetspeed, which describes itself as the largest independent fiber internet provider in the Northeast, began construction in Newport earlier in 2026. With the first connections in place, the company says it is now delivering service to its first Rhode Island market, according to What's Up Newp. The company operates in 11 states and is backed by the private equity firm Oak Hill Capital.

The numbers

The project combines a private investment from GoNetspeed with a grant from the Rhode Island Commerce Corporation's Capital Projects Fund, which draws on federal money from the U.S. Treasury. When finished, the network is expected to reach more than 8,200 homes and businesses in the city.

Fiber-optic lines carry data as pulses of light through glass strands, rather than as electrical signals over copper or coaxial cable. Providers generally use fiber to offer higher and more consistent speeds, and the same technology lets GoNetspeed sell matching upload and download speeds.

Service launches with symmetrical speeds, meaning upload and download speeds are the same, ranging from 500 megabits per second to 6 gigabits per second. The report did not include monthly prices or list which neighborhoods are connected first.

Who said what

GoNetspeed President and CEO Richard Clark said the company had originally committed to speeds of up to 2 gigabits in Newport and is going beyond that commitment by launching at up to 6 gigabits, according to What's Up Newp. No government officials were quoted in the announcement.

What it means for residents

For Newport households and businesses, the network adds a fiber option alongside existing cable and other internet service. Symmetrical speeds matter most for people who upload large files, work from home on video calls or run businesses that send data to the cloud. Residents can check whether service has reached their address on the company's Newport page at gonetspeed.com/ri/newport.

The state grant comes from the Capital Projects Fund, a federal program created to pay for broadband and other infrastructure that supports work, education and health monitoring. Rhode Island Commerce administers the state's share.

Newport is GoNetspeed's first Rhode Island market, so the city's buildout is also the company's first test in the state. The public share of the funding, $9 million, is more than twice the $3.9 million the company is putting in, according to the figures reported by What's Up Newp.

What comes next

Construction will continue as GoNetspeed works toward its goal of more than 8,200 locations. The company has also scheduled a no-charge seminar on switching from cable television to streaming services for Thursday, Dec. 3, from 5 to 7 p.m. at Innovate Newport. Registration is required, What's Up Newp reported.
