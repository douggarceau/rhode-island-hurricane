URI Plugs In: A New Applied AI Major Goes Live This Spring

The University of Rhode Island will soft-launch an Interdisciplinary and Applied AI bachelor's degree in spring 2027, and the first AI courses are already on the schedule.

The University of Rhode Island is building an artificial intelligence degree from the ground up, and the switch flips in a matter of weeks. According to URI's Institute for AI and Computational Research, a new Interdisciplinary and Applied AI bachelor's degree, known as IAI, will have a soft launch in spring 2027 and be fully integrated by fall 2027.

What it is

The institute's announcement, posted Oct. 27, says the degree is offered through URI's College of Business in cooperation with the College of Arts and Sciences. Students can take it as a stand-alone degree or as a second major alongside another field of study.

Per the announcement, students will learn to develop and apply AI tools responsibly, "with attention to reputable data sources, consent, safety, security, and privacy," and will work on interdisciplinary teams tackling real-world problems. A required internship is designed to give students experience working with companies as an AI specialist.

The university points graduates toward healthcare, life sciences, business and technology governance, with example paths that include AI ethics, human-AI interaction design, data analysis and entrepreneurship.

Who is behind it

The announcement names two leads: Indrani Mandal, an associate teaching professor in the Department of Computer Science and Statistics, and Devendra (Dave) Kale, interim associate dean for undergraduate education in the College of Business. The page describes a program that pairs "practical AI skills with ethical decision-making and collaboration across disciplines," and frames it as part of URI's growing investment in AI education and research, alongside the institute itself and existing AI programs in the business school.

The spring lineup

Students can begin earning credits toward the major in spring 2027, according to the institute, through business analytics and AI courses in the College of Business and through "AI for Everyone" (CSC/DSP 161) in the College of Arts and Sciences.

On Nov. 3 the institute also posted three applied AI courses for the spring semester, all running Jan. 26 to May 3, 2027:

AI in Marine & Earth Sciences (OCG 494-0012) is a three-credit, pass/fail course for juniors and seniors, and the listing says no coding background is required. It meets Mondays and Wednesdays from 3 to 4:15 p.m. in Bliss Hall 210.

AI Tools for Graduate Research in the Ocean, Environmental and Life Sciences (OCG 593) is aimed at graduate students and meets Mondays and Wednesdays from 9 to 10:15 a.m. in Watkins Hall 12 on the Narragansett Bay Campus.

AAI4STEM: Applications of AI Methods Across STEM Disciplines (PHY 591-0001) is open to graduate students and qualified advanced undergraduates with Python and quantitative preparation. It meets Tuesdays and Thursdays from 11 a.m. to 12:15 p.m. in Swan Hall 211.

Why it matters

The new major lands on a campus where AI work is already spreading fast. The institute's AI Lab report for the spring through fall 2026 semesters lists workshops on AI coding assistants and prompt engineering, an AI clinical learning coach for the pharmacy school built as a safe learning environment designed to protect patient privacy, and OceanDetect, a fish-detection project working at depths of 200 to 800 meters. The report also describes a legal-research study covering more than one million U.S. court opinions and counts 45 students served by the lab in fall 2026.

Outreach is reaching beyond the Kingston campus, too. The same report lists an AI and quantum computing summer camp for high school students and a winter course for the general public through URI's Osher Lifelong Learning Institute, "Understanding AI for Safe and Secure Use," which drew 45 participants.

For Rhode Island, that adds up to a homegrown pipeline: students who can learn the tools in Kingston and on the Bay Campus, then carry them into the state's hospitals, labs and companies through the required internship.

What comes next

Spring classes begin Jan. 26, 2027. The institute says registration details, instructor contacts and syllabus links for its applied AI courses are on its Applied AI Courses page, and the IAI announcement lists contact information for both program leads for students who want to get in early.
