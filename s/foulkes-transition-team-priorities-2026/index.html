Foulkes Names Transition Team, Plans Change at Top of Education Department

The governor-elect listed housing as her top priority and said she will not keep Education Commissioner Angélica Infante-Green.

Gov.-elect Helena Foulkes has named the transition team that will help shape her administration before she takes office on Jan. 5, and she used her first extended news briefing since the election to signal changes at the top of the state's education department, NBC 10 WJAR reported on Tuesday, Nov. 10.

Who is on the team

According to NBC 10, the transition is led by Bari Harlam, a business leader who worked with Foulkes at CVS, and Central Falls Mayor Maria Rivera. Other members include Johnston Mayor Joseph Polisena Jr.; David Cicilline, president of the Rhode Island Foundation and a former Providence mayor and congressman; state Sen. Lou DiPalma; Patrick Crowley, president of the Rhode Island AFL-CIO; and Paige Parks, executive director of Rhode Island KIDS COUNT.

Rhode Island Current reported on Nov. 6 that Foulkes had launched a transition website asking residents for ideas on improving state government and inviting applications from people who want to work in her administration, including on volunteer boards and commissions. NBC 10 reported that more than 700 Rhode Islanders had sent her ideas and feedback in the week after the election.

Priorities and the education commissioner

Foulkes said her priorities are lowering costs, expanding housing, creating good-paying jobs and improving education, with housing at the top of the list, NBC 10 reported. She said she plans to keep some current top officials but not Education Commissioner Angélica Infante-Green, saying she wants a different style of leadership that is more open and welcoming.

Infante-Green's contract runs through April 2028 at a salary of just under $300,000, according to NBC 10. Asked whether the state would pay out the contract to end it early, Foulkes said, "We'll figure that out."

Homelessness, energy bills and the bridge

Foulkes became emotional describing a weekend volunteer shift at a soup kitchen, where she said she saw many people with nowhere to sleep, NBC 10 reported. She called homelessness a leadership issue and said nonprofits working on it often compete rather than coordinate, a problem she wants the governor's office to address by bringing them together. She said she is considering more warming shelters this winter. The station noted that the McKee administration has spent tens of millions of dollars on homelessness.

She also said she plans to meet with the chief executive of Rhode Island Energy before Inauguration Day to press the company on delivery fees and on help for struggling customers. On the rebuilding of the westbound Washington Bridge, she said Rhode Islanders deserve better communication about the project.

The election backdrop

Foulkes, a Democrat, defeated Ken Block and Aaron Guckian in the Nov. 3 governor's race. Rhode Island Current reported that the election also changed the General Assembly and sent mixed results on borrowing.

Relations with the outgoing governor

Foulkes has not yet met with outgoing Gov. Dan McKee, whom she defeated in the September Democratic primary, NBC 10 reported. She said both of them had been busy, that their teams were working well together, and that she is open to a meeting.

What comes next

Foulkes is expected to continue naming members of her administration in the weeks ahead, and decisions about the education commissioner's contract and other top posts remain pending. Her planned meeting with Rhode Island Energy's chief executive is set to take place before she is sworn in on Jan. 5.
