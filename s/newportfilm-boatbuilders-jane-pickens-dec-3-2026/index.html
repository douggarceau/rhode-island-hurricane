Wood, Sweat and Sails: Sundance Winner Brings 'Boatbuilders' to Newport Dec. 3

newportFILM screens Luke Lorentzen's documentary about a Maine boat yard at the Jane Pickens, with the director and the IYRS president taking questions after.

A Sundance-winning filmmaker is bringing his new documentary to downtown Newport, and he is bringing the boat-yard questions with him. newportFILM will screen "Boatbuilders," Luke Lorentzen's portrait of a crew building a 56-foot wooden sailboat in Maine, on Thursday, Dec. 3 at the Jane Pickens Theater & Event Center, What'sUpNewp reported. After the film, Lorentzen will take questions on stage with Kern Maass, president of the IYRS School of Technology & Trades, the Newport school that teaches wooden boatbuilding.

What it is

"Boatbuilders" follows roughly 60 craftspeople at Brooklin Boat Yard in Maine as they build a 56-foot wooden sailboat from the ground up, according to What'sUpNewp. The film was shot across four seasons, and Lorentzen spent 16 months at the yard to make it. The finished documentary runs 103 minutes.

What'sUpNewp reports that the film premiered at the Venice International Film Festival this year. In a director's statement quoted in that report, Lorentzen calls the film "gripping and delightful" and says it is patient in its attention to detail; the outlet describes the film's aim as finding wonder in everyday routines.

Who is behind it

Lorentzen is no newcomer to the festival circuit. According to What'sUpNewp, he won the Sundance Film Festival's directing award for U.S. documentary in 2023 for "A Still Small Voice," and a Sundance special jury award for cinematography in 2019 for "Midnight Family."

The screening is a newportFILM event, presented with two local partners, IYRS and Sail Newport, What'sUpNewp reported. The outlet's photo caption frames the night as a celebration of craftsmanship, community and New England's maritime traditions.

The post-film conversation pairs the director with Maass, who leads IYRS. According to the report, the talk will cover the filmmaking, the craft itself and the case for preserving skilled trades.

The details

The screening is Thursday, Dec. 3, at the Jane Pickens Theater & Event Center, 49 Touro St., Newport. Doors open at 6 p.m. and the film starts at 7 p.m., What'sUpNewp reported. Tickets are $20 and are sold through newportFILM at newportfilm.com/films/boatbuilders/.

Why it matters

Newport is a city built around boats, and few screenings could land closer to home. A film about a working Maine boat yard, introduced by the head of the Newport school that trains the next generation of wooden-boat builders, turns a movie night into a conversation about a living local trade. It is also a chance to see a new festival documentary from a two-time Sundance winner, with the director in the room, on a downtown screen in early December.

For film fans, the format is the draw: a festival-season documentary, a big-screen showing and a live Q&A in the same evening, without leaving Aquidneck Island.

How to go

Buy tickets ahead through newportFILM's page for the film. Plan to arrive when doors open at 6 p.m. to settle in before the 7 p.m. start; with a 103-minute run time plus the conversation afterward, expect a full evening. The Jane Pickens is at 49 Touro St. in downtown Newport.
