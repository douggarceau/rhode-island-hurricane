David Morales, 28, Elected Providence Mayor With About 81 Percent of the Vote

The state representative becomes the youngest mayor in the city's history and has promised to bring back rent stabilization within his first 100 days.

State Rep. David Morales has been elected mayor of Providence, winning about 81 percent of the vote in the Nov. 3 general election and becoming, at 28, the youngest mayor in the city's history, according to The Boston Globe and Ocean State Media.

The results

Morales, a Democrat who describes himself as a democratic socialist, defeated Republican Dave Talan, the leader of the Providence Republican Party, who received about 17.2 percent, the Globe reported. The Associated Press called the race at 9:39 p.m. on election night.

Morales reached the general election by defeating incumbent Mayor Brett Smiley in the September Democratic primary. Rhode Island Current reported at the time that Morales won that race 57 percent to 43 percent in unofficial results, with citywide turnout of about 31 percent. Ocean State Media reported that Smiley did not endorse Morales in the general election.

Ocean State Media reported that Morales will be Providence's 40th mayor. The previous record for youngest mayor belonged to Joseph Paolino Jr., who took office in 1984; the Globe noted that Paolino was also 28 when he took office but turned 29 on his second day.

What Morales said

Speaking to NPR's All Things Considered, Morales said the city's residents voted overwhelmingly for the change they deserve. He told Ocean State Media his goal is to take on corporate interests and fight for working families, and said he is prepared for extra scrutiny as a young person of color.

The Globe reported that Morales said his campaign addressed working people's concerns, including rents, the school system and federal policies. He also said residents deserve a leader who is ready to explain his decisions.

What it means for residents

Housing is at the center of his agenda. Morales has pledged to sign a rent stabilization ordinance within his first 100 days, with rent stabilization in place by fall 2027 or early 2028, according to the Globe. Earlier this year, the City Council passed a proposal to cap annual increases on certain apartments at 4 percent; Smiley vetoed it, and an attempt to override the veto fell one vote short, Rhode Island Current reported. Smiley had argued that the measure did not address the root causes of the housing shortage.

Morales will also inherit a school system that has returned to city control after a seven-year state takeover, Ocean State Media reported. He has said he plans to end the city's Flock license-plate camera system. City voters also approved Question 7, a divestment measure that Morales supported, which the Globe said passed by a wide margin.

Who else is coming to City Hall

The new City Council term starts in January. Councilman Miguel Sanchez, also 28, is expected to be chosen council president, the Globe reported, which would make both the mayor and the council president democratic socialists for the first time.

What comes next

Morales has named June Rose, the City Council's chief of staff, and Jenn Stenfield to lead his transition team, and said he would name transition co-chairs, according to the Globe. He has said he plans to replace the city's public works director. Ocean State Media reported that he has spoken with Gov.-elect Helena Foulkes about housing and with members of the state's congressional delegation as he prepares to take office.
