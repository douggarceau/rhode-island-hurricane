RIDOT to Start Replacing Route 138 Railroad Bridge at Kingston Station Nov. 20

The two-phase South Kingstown project shifts traffic, closes part of Railroad Avenue and changes station access through the end of 2028.

The Rhode Island Department of Transportation will begin replacing the Route 138 railroad bridge at Kingston Station in South Kingstown later this month, a multi-year job that will change how drivers, rail passengers, bus riders and bike path users reach one of the state's busiest train stations. According to a RIDOT travel advisory issued Monday, Nov. 9, traffic changes start on Friday night, Nov. 20, and the bridge replacement is expected to be finished by the end of 2028.

What is happening

The bridge carries Route 138 over the railroad tracks beside Kingston Station, which is served by Amtrak trains and RIPTA buses. RIDOT said it will rebuild the bridge in two phases so the road can stay open throughout the work.

In the first phase, traffic will be shifted to the north side of the bridge while crews demolish and replace the southern half. RIDOT estimates that phase will last about one year. In the second phase, traffic will move onto the newly built southern section so the northern half can be replaced.

The bridge job is part of a larger Route 138 Corridor project that RIDOT estimates at $82.3 million. That project includes new sidewalks, upgraded traffic signals and repaving of Route 138 from Route 2, South County Trail, to Route 108, Kingstown Road. RIDOT said the corridor work is still in design. The department hopes to begin it later in 2027 and expects the full project to wrap up in spring 2029.

Changes for drivers

RIDOT said a temporary traffic signal and crosswalks will be installed at the intersection of Route 138 and Railroad Avenue. The section of Railroad Avenue that passes under Route 138 will be closed during the work.

The dedicated westbound left-turn lane onto Fairgrounds Road will also be removed for the duration of the project, according to the advisory. RIDOT said that lane will be restored once the bridge work is complete. A detour map is posted on the department's website at ridot.net/DetourMaps.

Getting to the station

All access to Kingston Station will be through the southern Railroad Avenue entrance, RIDOT said. To make that work, the department will remove the traffic island at that entrance so drivers traveling both eastbound and westbound on Route 138 can turn in.

People who park in the station's northern overflow lot will need to cross Route 138 at the new crosswalk and walk along Railroad Avenue to reach the platforms, according to the advisory. The work will take away 10 spaces from that overflow lot. RIDOT noted that a recent expansion added 129 spaces to the station, bringing its total to 344.

Users of the South County Bike Path may still park in the main lot, but RIDOT said they must park near the trailhead at the far end of the lot. The department also said University of Rhode Island parking is not permitted at the station.

The advisory did not describe any changes to Amtrak or RIPTA schedules.

What it means for residents

For South Kingstown and nearby communities, the project means more than two years of altered traffic patterns on a main route linking Route 1, the University of Rhode Island area and the train station. Commuters who use the station should plan for the single southern entrance and, if they park in the overflow lot, a longer walk.

What comes next

The traffic shift begins the night of Friday, Nov. 20. RIDOT cautioned that all schedules and project scope are subject to change based on conditions, findings and weather. Updated detour information will be posted on the department's detour map page.
