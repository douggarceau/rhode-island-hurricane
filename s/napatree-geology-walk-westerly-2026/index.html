Uncovering Napatree: A Geology Walk on Westerly's Shore, Nov. 14

Saturday, Nov. 14 from 9 to 10:30 a.m. at Napatree Point Conservation Area, Fort Road, Westerly; registration is required.

Napatree Point is a long, narrow spit of sand and stone at the western end of Westerly's shoreline, and on Saturday, Nov. 14 The Watch Hill Conservancy invites visitors to learn how it was built. The Uncovering Napatree walk runs from 9 to 10:30 a.m. at the Napatree Point Conservation Area on Fort Road.

The walk is led by Dr. Bryan Oakley, a geoscientist who sits on the Conservancy's Science Advisory Team and teaches Environmental Earth Sciences at Eastern Connecticut State University. He will explain the forces that have shaped the point, how longshore drift and major storms continually reshape its sand, and how the geology supports the area's resilience as climate conditions change.

What to expect

The program lasts about 90 minutes and takes place on foot. Parking is available along Bay Street. The organizers say that rain will cancel the program. If that happens, the rain date is Sunday, Nov. 15 from 9 to 10 a.m., and cancellations are announced by email to those who registered.

Registration is required and can be completed through the Conservancy's event page at thewatchhillconservancy.org. The walk is offered at no charge, and it is a good chance to see how science and stewardship work together on one of the state's most loved beaches.
