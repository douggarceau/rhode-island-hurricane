Four Strings, Two Towns: Newport String Quartet Plays Tiverton and Newport

Haydn, Debussy and a 2013 Swedish quartet at the Four Corners Meeting House on Dec. 5 and the Newport Art Museum on Dec. 6.

Haydn, Debussy and a Swedish quartet written in 2013 are heading to two of Newport County's most atmospheric rooms on the first weekend of December. The Newport String Quartet will play a Saturday afternoon concert at the Four Corners Meeting House in Tiverton on Dec. 5 and repeat the program on Sunday, Dec. 6 at the Newport Art Museum, What'sUpNewp reported.

What it is

The two afternoons are the second installment of the Newport County Concert Series, according to What'sUpNewp. Both concerts start at 2 p.m. and share the same program, so audiences on either side of the county can catch the same set close to home.

The program opens with Haydn's String Quartet in B minor, Op. 64 No. 2. In the middle sits String Quartet No. 2, "Madárdal," a 2013 work by Swedish composer Andrea Tarrodi. Debussy's String Quartet closes the afternoon, the outlet reported.

Who is behind it

The Newport String Quartet is the resident ensemble of the Newport String Project. Its players, per What'sUpNewp, are violinists Ealaín McMullin and Kenneth Trotter, violist Florrie Marshall and cellist Chelsea Bernstein.

Tickets are sold through the Newport String Project at newportstringproject.org, What'sUpNewp reported; the outlet did not list prices.

The Sunday concert at the Newport Art Museum takes place alongside the museum's current exhibition of Sheila Isham's "Between Worlds," according to the report, so a ticket to the music also puts you in the building with that show.

Why it matters

This is a compact program with real range: two names every classical fan knows, Haydn and Debussy, bracketing a 2013 piece by a living Swedish composer that most listeners will be hearing live for the first time. Slotting a contemporary work between two classics is the kind of programming that keeps chamber music feeling current rather than dusty, and it gives a casual listener an easy way in.

The venues help, too. A meeting house on Main Road in Tiverton and a museum gallery on Bellevue Avenue are intimate rooms, the kind of spaces where four string players can fill the air without amplification and audiences sit close enough to watch the bows.

How to go

Pick your side of the county: Tiverton on Saturday or Newport on Sunday, both at 2 p.m. Buy tickets in advance at newportstringproject.org. If you choose Sunday, budget extra time to walk through the Isham exhibition at the museum before or after the music. Both afternoons wrap before dark, leaving room for an early dinner out on the first weekend of December.
