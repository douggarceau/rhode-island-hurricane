Central Falls High School Says Its Phone Rules Are Paying Off Under State Ban

A city that limited cell phones in class before the state acted says stricter enforcement this year is changing how students learn and talk.

Central Falls High School, which began limiting student cell phone use in 2023, is seeing more conversation in its cafeteria and better focus in its classrooms now that a statewide ban on phones in schools is backing up its rules, according to a report published Nov. 17 by Ocean State Media. Rhode Island this year joined a growing list of states banning cell phones in schools, and most districts are still adjusting to the change.

What happened

Central Falls put restrictions on phones in place three years ago, before the state law, Ocean State Media reported. This school year, enforcement is stricter. Staff members stand at the school entrance to help students put their phones away as they arrive. Students who break the rules face a series of escalating consequences, and after a certain point a parent must come to the school to pick up the device.

A photo caption in the report said schools in Central Falls temporarily lock student electronics in pouches made by the company Yondr. Students can still reach their parents through the front office in an emergency, the report said.

The background

Rhode Island lawmakers passed the cell phone legislation in 2025, according to Ocean State Media, and the ban is now in effect in schools across the state. Central Falls is one of several districts that had local rules first.

What staff and students said

English teacher Emmanuel Ramos told Ocean State Media that the policy is working better than ever and gave the state law credit for the improvement. He described phone distraction as a major problem after the pandemic, noting that Central Falls had some of the longest stretches of remote learning in the state. Before strong rules existed, he said, it was very hard to pull students away from their screens.

Ramos said he now hears more talk among students at lunch and sees easier teamwork during group projects. He said students who tend to tune out can no longer retreat into their devices as easily, and that he has a clearer picture of what each student is actually learning. He also acknowledged that some students still look for ways around the rules.

A student interviewed for the report said the policy is a good idea even though going without a phone can be frustrating. She said the change has helped her get to know classmates outside her usual group of friends.

What it means for families

For parents across Rhode Island, the Central Falls experience offers an early look at how the statewide ban may play out. The main lesson Ramos offered other districts was consistency: he warned that letting some students keep their phones, or not responding when students sneak them in, weakens the effort.

What comes next

Ocean State Media did not report any new changes to the Central Falls policy. Districts across the state will continue putting the state ban into practice through the rest of the school year.
