Rhode Island Energy Plans Deeper Bill Discounts of Up to 75% Next Year

A new income-based discount from the state's largest utility would cut some electric bills by as much as three-quarters, starting in 2027.

Rhode Island Energy says a new Enhanced Electric Discount Rate program could cut eligible customers' electric bills by as much as 75%, a significant step up from the discounts the utility offers today, NBC 10 News reported Monday. The program is expected to begin next year.

What is changing

Under the plan described by the utility, the size of a household's discount would depend on its income, with the lowest-income households receiving the largest cuts. NBC 10 reported five discount levels: eligible households would receive 10%, 38%, 56%, 66% or 75% off their bills.

Customers who already receive help through programs such as SNAP, Medicaid or Supplemental Security Income may qualify, according to the report. The station said the utility had not yet explained how customers will enroll, and that it has many unanswered questions about the program that it plans to raise with the company.

How it compares with today's discount

Rhode Island Energy's current discount rate program, as described on the company's website, offers two flat levels. Customers enrolled in SNAP, the LIHEAP heating assistance program or SSI receive 25% off their monthly bill. Customers enrolled in Medicaid, the Rhode Island Works program or public assistance receive 30% off.

The top tier of the new program would more than double the largest discount available under the current structure, while the lowest tier would be smaller than either of today's flat discounts. NBC 10 did not report how many customers are expected to land in each tier or what income levels the tiers would use.

The backdrop

The announcement comes as electric bills remain a heated issue in Rhode Island. NBC 10 reported that protesters went to the Public Utilities Commission over the weekend to demand more transparency from Rhode Island Energy.

Affordability has been a recurring topic at the State House as well. In February, a group of state representatives introduced House bill 7880, which proposed requiring large electric and gas utilities to file a tiered, income-based payment plan with the Public Utilities Commission for households at or below 150% of the federal poverty level. The bill text, posted by the General Assembly, called for discounts sized so that eligible customers would spend no more than a set share of their income on utility costs. The NBC 10 report did not connect the bill to the utility's new program.

What it means for residents

For households that qualify for the deepest discount, a 75% reduction could mean paying a quarter of a typical bill. Households that are enrolled in assistance programs now and want to keep their current discount can still apply through the existing process, which the company's website says involves submitting an application and a confirmation letter from an eligible program. The Rhode Island Department of Human Services helps residents get those letters at 855-697-4347.

What comes next

The program is expected to start in 2027. Details still to come include how customers sign up, the income limits for each tier, and whether current discount customers will be moved into the new structure automatically. Residents can watch Rhode Island Energy's assistance programs page and Public Utilities Commission dockets for updates.
