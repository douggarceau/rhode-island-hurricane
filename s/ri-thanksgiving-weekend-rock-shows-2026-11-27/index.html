Skip the Mall, Hit the Club: Rhode Island's Thanksgiving Weekend Shows

Shaun Cassidy in Cranston, ska at The Parlour and punk at Ocean Mist lead a loud Friday and Saturday across the state.

The turkey is carved, the dishes are done and the couch is calling. Ignore it. Rhode Island's clubs and theaters have a stacked Thanksgiving weekend lined up, from a 1970s teen idol in Cranston to ska veterans and punk rock on both ends of the state, according to JamBase's statewide concert listings.

Friday and Saturday carry the load. JamBase lists six Rhode Island shows across the two nights, spread from downtown Providence to the South County shore, and nothing on the books for Thanksgiving night itself. Here is how the weekend shakes out.

Friday: a teen idol and a ska stomp

The biggest name on Friday, Nov. 27, is Shaun Cassidy at the Park Theatre in Cranston, per JamBase. If you only know him from your parents' record collection, here is the short version: ABC7 in Los Angeles reported last December that Cassidy signed a record deal while still in high school, put out his first solo album in 1977 and took "Da Doo Ron Ron" to No. 1. He also starred in "The Hardy Boys/Nancy Drew Mysteries" on TV, then spent about three decades writing and producing television, including creating "American Gothic" and working as executive producer and writer on "New Amsterdam."

His comeback tour, "The Road to Us," was his first concert tour in decades, ABC7 reported, and he described it as a storytelling show that mixes the hits with new songs and stories about his life and family. "It's kind of a live memoir," Cassidy told the station. Whether the Cranston night follows that same format is worth checking with the Park Theatre before you go.

Over in Providence the same night, The Parlour hosts Bim Skala Bim, with So Many Dangers opening, according to the JamBase listing. If your Black Friday plan was a mall parking lot a ska band in a small club is the better call.

Saturday: punk by the beach, rock downtown

Saturday, Nov. 28, is the bigger night. JamBase lists I Am the Avalanche at Ocean Mist in South Kingstown putting a loud punk night on the South County shore.

Back in the capital, The Agents play Alchemy, and The Parlour returns with The Birkitt Transmissions, backed by John Larson & The Silver Fields and Chris Principe and the Forgotten, per JamBase. Three bands on one bill is an easy way to hear a cross-section of the scene in a single night.

And the arena crowd has its own option: Nicky Jam brings his Perreoland show to Amica Mutual Pavilion the same night for the weekend's arena-sized option.

Why it matters

Thanksgiving weekend is when everybody's home: college kids, old bandmates, the friends who moved to Brooklyn. The clubs know it, and the calendar shows it, with shows booked in three different towns over two nights. It is also a reminder that the Rhode Island scene is not just Providence. Cranston's Park Theatre and South Kingstown's Ocean Mist pull their weight too.

How to go

JamBase does not list showtimes or prices for these shows, so check each venue's own page or ticket link before heading out, and buy ahead for the Park Theatre and Amica, where seats go first. For the smaller rooms, doors and set times can shift, so a quick check of the club's social pages on the day is smart. Then go burn off the stuffing.
