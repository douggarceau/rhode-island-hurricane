Providence's Recreation Centers: What They Offer and What's Still Coming

The city runs about a dozen recreation sites in 11 neighborhoods, with Elmwood newly reopened and adult programs still in development.

Providence's Recreation Department operates about a dozen recreation facilities in 11 of the city's 25 neighborhoods, drawing roughly 500 visitors a day, according to city data cited in a report published this week by The Providence Eye. The report, based on visits to several centers this fall, found strong youth programming across most sites but few options for adults, and the city says plans for adult programs are still being developed.

What the centers offer

Most of the city's centers run bowling and basketball in the fall, according to the report. A smaller set of sports, including indoor soccer, volleyball, swimming and cheerleading, is offered only at certain centers.

All programs run by the department are offered at no charge or at low cost for Providence residents, the Eye reported. Schedules are posted on the city's RecDesk online portal, and the city encourages families to sign up there, saying registration helps with staffing and communication.

A look at individual sites

The West End Recreation Center on Bucklin Street hosts basketball and other youth programs. Parents interviewed by the Eye described a familiar, welcoming atmosphere and praised the staff.

The Batastini Recreation Center on Obadiah Brown Road, set behind a school next to a golf course, has the only indoor pool in the city's recreation system. It offers swim classes for youth and adults for $5 and hosts a weekly women's basketball session for ages 16 and older. It was the only visited site offering adult programming this fall.

The Vincent Brown Recreation Center, at Hope Street and Doyle Avenue, has bleachers in its gym, a weight room and a padded mat room used for wrestling.

The Kennedy Recreation Center normally operates inside Robert F. Kennedy Elementary School on Nelson Street through a partnership with Providence Public Schools. While a new Kennedy school is built, its members are using Esek Hopkins Middle School on Charles Street.

The Elmwood Recreation Center reopened earlier this year after closing in 2019 for renovations paid for in part by a state Community Learning Center grant. It does not yet have scheduled programs but offers open gym six days a week.

What officials said

A city spokesperson, responding to the Eye by email, said the city values adult sports leagues and the opportunities they give residents, and that plans for adult programs remain in development. No timeline was given.

What it means for residents

For families, the centers remain a low-cost option for after-school and weekend activities, especially in the colder months. Parents told the Eye that the staff and the sense of community keep them coming back. For adults, the options are limited for now: aside from the swim classes and women's basketball at Batastini, the visited centers had no fall adult programming.

What comes next

The city has said more programs are expected at Elmwood, though none had been announced. Kennedy's programs will stay at Esek Hopkins until the new elementary school is finished. Funding for recreation staff and programs is set each year in the city budget.
