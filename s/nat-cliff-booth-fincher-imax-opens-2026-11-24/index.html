Cliff Booth Is Back, and He Only Plays in IMAX for Two Weeks

David Fincher directs Brad Pitt in a Quentin Tarantino script that picks up eight years after Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, opening in IMAX Wednesday.

The short version

Brad Pitt's stuntman with a past returns Wednesday. The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth opens in IMAX theaters worldwide on Nov. 25 for an exclusive two-week run, then lands on Netflix on Dec. 23, according to Netflix and JoBlo.

Who made it

The pairing is the hook. David Fincher directs from a new script by Quentin Tarantino, Netflix reports. Pitt produces with Ceán Chaffin. Fincher regulars fill out the crew, including cinematographer Erik Messerschmidt, editor Kirk Baxter and sound designer Ren Klyce. Netflix lists the runtime at 2 hours 23 minutes.

JoBlo notes the final trailer, released last week, is the first time Netflix has put both Tarantino's and Fincher's names in its marketing for the film.

The story

It is 1977, eight years after the 1969 events of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. Cliff is working as a fixer. Per Netflix's synopsis, he is hired through talent agent Karen Crosby, played by Elizabeth Debicki, to handle problems for TV star Charlie Peters, played by Scott Caan.

The cast also includes Carla Gugino as Toni, Yahya Abdul-Mateen II as Roderick "Hot Rod" Harrison, Peter Weller, Corey Fogelmanis and Karren Karagulian, with Matt Groove and JB Tadena as the Verdusco brothers.

Why the release plan matters

This is a Netflix movie getting a theatrical run first, and only on IMAX screens, worldwide, starting the day before Thanksgiving. If you want the big-screen version, the window is short: the IMAX engagement lasts two weeks, per Netflix, and the film arrives on the streamer about four weeks after it opens.

The holiday crowd

It walks into a packed Thanksgiving week at the movies, where the IMAX screens are the prize. For Fincher fans, it is also a change of pace: a 1970s Hollywood hangout story built on a character Tarantino created, rather than another procedural thriller. For Tarantino fans, it is a chance to see his writing in someone else's hands for the first time on this scale.

Early word is warm. A JoBlo review calls it Fincher's most fun movie since Gone Girl.
