Newport's The Elms Opens a Multimillion-Dollar Immersive Experience

The Preservation Society of Newport County has turned the Gilded Age mansion into a multi-sensory museum about the Berwind family and the staff who ran the house.

The Elms, one of Newport's best-known Gilded Age mansions, has launched a new immersive exhibit that uses projections, sound and even scent to tell the story of the family who lived there and the workers who kept the house running. Ocean State Media reported that The Elms Experience launched Monday and is believed to be the first installation of its kind at a historic house in the United States.

What Happened

The Preservation Society of Newport County, which operates The Elms, produced the exhibit with Digital Kitchen, an Emmy-winning creative studio, according to Ocean State Media. The project took about three years and a crew of roughly 150 people, including designers, historians, writers, engineers and voice actors, according to Melanie Garcia, the society's director of visitor experience. The society described it only as a multimillion-dollar project and did not give an exact cost.

What Visitors Will See

The exhibit opens more rooms to guests than before, with fewer barriers. In the Ballroom, five projected displays show dancers set to ambient music. The Rose Room features a projection of a chambermaid at work, and the ceiling of the Butler's Pantry shows the shadows and sounds of the Berwind family's pet monkeys. A custom orange scent fills the Conservatory, and the Dining Room table is set for a dinner party of 12, where visitors can listen in on an imagined conversation.

Other features include short videos, QR codes with more information in each room, a viewer that shows how the gardens changed over time and an animated oil painting of Edward Berwind narrating his own history. Ocean State Media reported that speakers were built into the paneling, rugs were made to match the floors and screens were hung so they would not damage the historic walls.

Who Said What

Garcia told Ocean State Media that tourism and interest in historic house museums have been declining, even with the popularity of HBO's The Gilded Age, much of which was filmed inside The Elms. She said the goal is to reach younger and more diverse audiences, including people who might not otherwise visit a mansion. She said the technology was designed to blend into the house and meet the highest historic standards, and she noted that The Elms was among the first fully electrified houses in Newport in 1901.

What It Means for Residents

For Newport, where the mansions are a major draw for visitors, the exhibit is a bid to bring new audiences through the doors during the off-season and beyond. Tickets cost $35, according to Ocean State Media. The article did not list hours, which visitors can check with the Preservation Society before going.

What Comes Next

Garcia said the installation is complete but was built to change as new research and technology become available, so visitors have a reason to return. The Preservation Society did not announce plans for similar exhibits at its other properties.
