Potter Street Bridge Comes Down Over I-195 in East Providence

An overnight demolition closed Interstate 195 in both directions as RIDOT clears the way for more highway lanes and a new bridge.

Crews removed the Potter Street Bridge over Interstate 195 in East Providence over the weekend, closing the highway in both directions overnight Friday into Saturday, according to the Rhode Island Department of Transportation and NBC 10 News. RIDOT pushed back the planned morning reopening so it could open every lane at once.

What happened

Both directions of I-195 in East Providence closed late Friday, Nov. 6, so the bridge could be taken down. RIDOT said in posts on X that demolition crews worked through the night and that the bridge deck was down shortly after 2 a.m. Saturday. Cleanup of the debris continued into Saturday morning.

The department had planned to open some lanes at 10 a.m. Saturday. Citing the weather, RIDOT adjusted that plan so crews could reopen all lanes together, and said the work was trending ahead of a noon target.

Detours and traffic

During the closure, traffic was sent along a Warren Avenue detour. RIDOT said traffic on the detour was flowing well, but it urged drivers to avoid the area if they could while crews finished the work.

Why the bridge came down

According to NBC 10, the bridge was removed so that more lanes can be added to I-195 through the area. A new Potter Street Bridge will be built in its place. The report did not give a cost or a completion date for the replacement span, and it did not name any officials beyond the department.

What it means for residents

I-195 is the main highway link between East Providence, Providence and southeastern Massachusetts, and the Potter Street crossing connects neighborhoods on either side of the interstate. Until the replacement bridge opens, people who used Potter Street to get across the highway will need to use other crossings. Drivers on I-195 can expect continued construction activity in the area as the lane additions move forward.

The work adds to a long stretch of highway projects on the East Providence side of the Seekonk River, where commuters have also lived with the rebuilding of the westbound Washington Bridge.

What comes next

With the old bridge gone, the next steps are building the new Potter Street Bridge and widening the highway beneath it. RIDOT typically announces lane shifts and overnight closures in advance on its website and social media accounts, and drivers who use the corridor can check there before heading out.
