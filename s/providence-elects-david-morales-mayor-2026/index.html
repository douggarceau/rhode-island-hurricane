Providence Elects David Morales, 28, as Its Youngest Mayor

The state representative won about 81 percent of the vote and says rent stabilization will be among his first priorities when he takes office in January.

Providence voters elected state Rep. David Morales as the city's next mayor on Nov. 3. The Associated Press called the race at 9:39 p.m., and Morales received about 81 percent of the vote to 17.2 percent for Republican Dave Talan, the Boston Globe reported.

Morales, a 28-year-old Democrat who describes himself as a democratic socialist, will be the 40th mayor of Providence and the youngest in its history, according to Ocean State Media. Joseph Paolino Jr. was also 28 when he took office in 1984, but turned 29 on his second day, the Globe noted.

How he got here

Morales won the September Democratic primary over Mayor Brett Smiley by about 5 percentage points. Smiley did not endorse him for the general election, Ocean State Media reported. Talan is a frequent candidate and leads the Providence Republican Party.

Morales has represented House District 7, which covers the Mount Pleasant, Valley and Elmhurst neighborhoods, since 2021. He was elected to the legislature at 21 and holds a master's degree in public affairs from Brown University.

His priorities

Housing was the center of his campaign. Morales has said he plans to sign a rent stabilization ordinance within his first 100 days. A previous version, which Smiley vetoed, would have capped yearly rent increases at 4 percent, with exemptions. City Council leaders are expected to introduce a new policy in January, and Morales has said he hopes to have rent stabilization in place by fall 2027 or early 2028, the Globe reported. He has also said he wants to build more affordable housing.

He will take over a school system that is returning to city control after a seven-year state takeover, and he has said he supports no-charge bus service for schoolchildren. He also said he plans to replace the director of the Department of Public Works after problems with snow removal last winter.

Morales told supporters he was proud to have run a campaign focused on the concerns of working people, including rents and public schools, and he said the city will face difficult choices. The Globe described an uncertain financial outlook for the city.

Other results

Providence voters also approved Question 7, a binding measure that requires the city to divest from entities that profit from genocide, apartheid, ethnic cleansing and prolonged military occupation, according to unofficial results. The campaign for it was driven largely by the group Providence for Palestine, Ocean State Media reported. Morales supported the measure; Smiley opposed it.

City Councilor Miguel Sanchez, also 28, is expected to be chosen council president by his colleagues when the new term begins in January, the Globe reported.

What comes next

Morales' transition team is led by June Rose, chief of staff to the City Council, and Jenn Stenfield. He has spoken with Gov.-elect Helena Foulkes and members of the congressional delegation about housing. The new city term begins in January.
