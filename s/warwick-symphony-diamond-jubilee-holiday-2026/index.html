Warwick Symphony Orchestra Marks 60 Years With a Holiday Celebration

Sunday, Dec. 13, 3 to 4 p.m., at St. Bernard Church in North Kingstown, with Catherine Gagnon conducting

The Warwick Symphony Orchestra is marking its 60th anniversary with a holiday celebration on Sunday, Dec. 13, from 3 to 4 p.m. at St. Bernard Church, 275 Tower Hill Road in North Kingstown. The program is billed as the Diamond Jubilee concert, and it brings a full orchestra into a church setting for an afternoon of seasonal music that the ensemble offers to communities across the state.

Catherine Gagnon, the orchestra's music director, leads the ensemble. Michael Raymond joins as the recorder soloist in A Basque Carol, arranged by Chris Thomas. The rest of the program moves through familiar seasonal favorites: Morton Gould's Adeste Fideles, Bob Krogstad's arrangement of The Bells of Christmas, Alfred Reed's Russian Christmas Music, Carl Strommen's There's Christmas in the Air, and Mother Ginger from Tchaikovsky's Nutcracker, arranged by Sandra Dackow.

A Holiday Finish

The concert closes with A Vaughan Williams Christmas, arranged by Douglas Wagner, a set of carols that suits both a holiday audience and a church sanctuary. Programs like this are a good way to hear a symphony orchestra at close range in a relaxed setting, with the whole family in the pews.

Tickets and Admission

Advance tickets are sold through ON THE STAGE at $20 general admission and $15 for seniors and students with ID. At the door the prices are $23 general and $18 for seniors and students with ID. Children under 12 are admitted at no charge and do not need a ticket. Online purchases carry additional ticketing fees.

The Warwick Symphony Orchestra describes itself as a regional volunteer orchestra that aims to offer high-quality live music at prices that keep it within reach. The concert is supported in part by the Rhode Island State Council on the Arts and the National Endowment for the Arts. Organizers suggest that families arrive a little early to settle in before the first downbeat.
