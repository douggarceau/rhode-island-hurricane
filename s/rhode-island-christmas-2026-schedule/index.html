Rhode Island's 2026 Christmas Season Events Guide

Dates, times and prices for Trinity Rep, Cirque du Soleil, the Boston Pops, The Nutcracker, the Newport mansions, tree lightings and light displays statewide.

Rhode Island's Christmas season stretches from early November theater performances to illuminated gardens and mansion grounds that stay open into January. This year's calendar includes Trinity Rep's 50th-anniversary A Christmas Carol, Cirque du Soleil's holiday show in Providence, the Boston Pops, Ballet RI's The Nutcracker, waterfront tree lightings and several large outdoor light displays.

This schedule was checked against each organizer's own page on Nov. 5, 2026. All times are Eastern. Where an organizer has not yet published a time, the story says so. Listed prices may not include ticketing fees, and schedules can change.

Trinity Rep's A Christmas Carol: Nov. 5–Dec. 31, Providence

Trinity Repertory Company marks 50 years of its holiday tradition with a new production in the Dowling Theater at 201 Washington St. Company member Taavon Gamble directs and choreographs, Mauro Hantman plays Ebenezer Scrooge, and the production uses Richard Cumming's original music.

Performance times vary by date, so check Trinity Rep's ticket calendar. The theater lists its best availability as Nov. 14–25 in November, and Tuesdays, Wednesdays, Thursdays and Dec. 26–31 in December. A Sensory-Friendly Plus! performance is Saturday, Nov. 21, at noon.

The Polar Express: weekends Nov. 6–Dec. 20, Woonsocket

The historic train depot at One Depot Square is the starting point for a roughly three-hour outing built around The Polar Express, including a 90-minute train ride, a reading of the story, songs, games, hot chocolate, a cookie and a visit from Santa. Each child receives a silver bell.

Choose a date and start time through the booking calendar, or call the box office at (401) 495-1213. The organizer says tickets sell out quickly.

Holidays at the Newport Mansions: Nov. 21–Jan. 3

The Breakers, Marble House and Chateau-sur-Mer are decorated for the season, with nearly 30 Christmas trees themed to their rooms. The Breakers' Great Hall holds a 15-foot tree made of 150 poinsettia plants.

Sparkling Lights at The Breakers: selected nights, Newport

This evening event pairs an illuminated outdoor walk with the decorated mansion in one visit. Entry is timed at 4, 4:30, 5, 5:30 and 6 p.m., and the house and grounds close at 8 p.m. It requires its own timed ticket.

The dates are Nov. 21, 22, 27, 28 and 29, and Dec. 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 18, 20, 21, 22, 23, 26 and 27. Nonmember prices are $45 for adults and $18 for ages 6–12. Member prices are $30 for adults and $18 for ages 6–17. Children under 6 enter at no charge.

Christmas at Blithewold and Sparkle!: Nov. 24–Jan. 3, Bristol

Blithewold's 2026 theme at 101 Ferry Road is "Gifts of Nature," with birds, butterflies, mushrooms, flowers and storybook scenes drawn from the estate's gardens, woodlands and shoreline. The manor and grounds are open 11 a.m.–7 p.m., generally Tuesday through Sunday, and closed Nov. 26 and Dec. 24–25.

The evening Sparkle! program runs 4–7 p.m., with last admission at 7 p.m. and closing at 8 p.m. Prices are $27 for nonmember adults and $8 for nonmember ages 6–17, or $15 and $5 for members. Children 5 and under enter at no charge. S'mores kits are $5 on Friday and Saturday nights.

Cirque du Soleil, 'Twas the Night Before...: Nov. 24–Dec. 6, Providence

Cirque du Soleil brings its holiday production and a cast of 26 artists to the Providence Performing Arts Center, 220 Weybosset St.

Holiday Lights Spectacular: Nov. 27–Jan. 3, Providence

Roger Williams Park Zoo's walk has more than 3.5 million lights across 40 acres on a path of just over a mile. It runs 5–9:30 p.m. nightly, with final admission at 9 p.m., rain or shine, and is closed Dec. 24 and 25. Tickets must be reserved online: $15 for adults, $13 for ages 2–12, and no charge for ages 1 and under. Tuesday tickets are $5 off.

The Santa Parade is Friday, Dec. 4, 5:30–6 p.m. Santa visits are Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, Dec. 4–20, 6–8 p.m. Character meet-and-greets are Mondays and Wednesdays, 5:30–7:30 p.m.

Rhode Island Lights Festival: Nov. 27–Jan. 3, Pawtucket

Centreville Bank Stadium hosts this first-time festival, with light displays on the field and around the stadium, synchronized shows through the seating bowl, a holiday marketplace, food, s'mores and visits with Santa. It runs nightly 5–10 p.m. and is closed Dec. 24 and 25. The announcement does not list individual ticket prices, so check with the stadium.

Wickford Festival of Lights: Dec. 3–6, North Kingstown

Wickford Village's 41st annual festival opens Thursday, Dec. 3, with the tree lighting at Updike Park. Santa arrives by boat at the Town Dock on Brown Street on Friday evening, Saturday afternoon and Sunday afternoon, led by kayaking "reindeer," with carols and a horse-drawn hayride through the village. Saturday adds an Elf Parade, and many shops stay open until 9 p.m. Friday and Saturday. The village association has not yet posted clock times for those events.

PVD Holiday Festival: Dec. 3–6, Providence

The Downcity Holiday Market runs Dec. 3–6 at Grant's Block Park. The Illuminate PVD Holiday Stroll is Friday, Dec. 4, at 6 p.m., leading to the market. The City Hall tree lighting is Saturday, Dec. 5; the festival's page does not list a start time.

Jamestown Holiday Stroll and Tree Lighting: Dec. 5

The Village Holiday Stroll runs 9 a.m.–5 p.m., with craft fairs and business open houses. Santa and Mrs. Claus arrive by boat at East Ferry Wharf at 1 p.m., then ride a fire truck to the Conanicut Gift Shop for photos. The tree lighting is 5:30–7 p.m. at East Ferry Wharf, and Boy Scouts place luminaries along the East Ferry seawall and Narragansett Avenue.

The Jamestown Arts Center, 18 Valley St., holds its Holiday Arts Market Saturday, 10 a.m.–4 p.m., with a pre-party and early shopping Friday, Dec. 4, 5:30–7:30 p.m.

Bowen's Wharf Tree Lighting: Dec. 5, Newport

The 56th annual event begins around 12:30 p.m., when Santa and Mrs. Claus arrive by boat in Bowen's Basin, weather permitting. The tree is lit at 6 p.m. Admission is at no charge.

Bristol Grand Illumination and Christmas Festival: Dec. 5–6

The town lists the Grand Illumination for Saturday, Dec. 5, at the Burnside Building, Hope and Court streets, with a Dec. 6 rain date. The Christmas Festival is the same weekend. Neither has a confirmed 2026 start time yet.

Pawtucket Winter Wonderland: Dec. 5–6 and 12–13

Slater Park's Winter Wonderland runs 4–8 p.m. on Dec. 5, 6, 12 and 13, on the grounds near the Slater Park Carousel. The city's calendar lists hundreds of decorated trees, a miniature village, a giant talking snowman, carousel, train and horse-drawn hay rides, and visits and photos with Santa.

WaterFire Holiday Basin Lighting: Dec. 11, Providence

Onshore programming and vendors open at 4 p.m. at Waterplace Park. Sunset is 4:14 p.m., and the lighting ends at 9 p.m.

Boston Pops: Dec. 13, Providence

Keith Lockhart leads the Boston Pops Esplanade Orchestra at the Providence Performing Arts Center at 3 and 7 p.m. The 3 p.m. matinee was added due to demand. The program includes "Sleigh Ride," new arrangements of seasonal favorites, a sing-along and an appearance by Santa, and runs about two hours with one intermission.

Handel's Messiah: Dec. 13, Providence

The Rhode Island Philharmonic performs at The VETS, One Avenue of the Arts, at 3 p.m., with doors at 2 p.m. Christine Noel conducts, with soprano Maya Kherani, mezzo-soprano Emily Marvosh, tenor Jonas Budris and baritone Andrew Garland. Tickets start at $25 and are sold by the Philharmonic at (401) 248-7000, not the VETS box office. This concert overlaps the Pops matinee.

Ballet RI's The Nutcracker: Dec. 17–27, Providence

Yury Yanowsky's production plays The VETS. Tickets are $36–$130, and a $65 Meet the Stars add-on is available at the matinees.

WaterFire Starts at Sunset: Dec. 31, Providence

New Year's Eve closes the calendar at the Michael S. Van Leesten Memorial Bridge, 365 S. Water St. WaterFire's event page lists a 5 p.m. start and a 9 p.m. end, with the bridge lighting and torch procession at 5:30 p.m., as part of Providence's citywide celebration, which ends with fireworks at 8:30 p.m.
