Foulkes Faults $2.2M Airport CEO Deal, Plans Review of Quasi-Public Pay

The governor-elect asked quasi-public boards to hold off on executive contracts after the airport board roughly doubled its chief's annual pay.

Governor-elect Helena Foulkes said Monday she will order a full review of executive pay at Rhode Island's quasi-public agencies once she takes office, after the board of the Rhode Island Airport Corporation approved a contract that roughly doubles the annual compensation of its president and chief executive, Iftikhar Ahmad, according to NBC 10 News. Foulkes also asked every quasi-public board to put off decisions on executive compensation and contract extensions until her administration begins on Jan. 5.

What happened

The airport corporation's board approved a new three-year contract for Ahmad on Thursday, Nov. 12, NBC 10's I-Team reported. Ahmad has led T.F. Green International Airport and the state's five other airports for a decade, and the contract is retroactive to Oct. 13, his 10th anniversary in the job. The airport system also includes Block Island, Newport, Quonset, Westerly and North Central airports.

According to NBC 10, the deal brings Ahmad's total yearly pay to about $2.19 million. A year earlier, the station reported, Ahmad signed a contract with a base salary just under $528,000 and total pay of around $900,000. NBC 10 noted the money comes from airport revenue, not from taxpayer funds.

NBC 10 also reported that the contract allows 28 vacation days, eight personal days and 24 sick days a year, adding up to as much as 12 paid weeks off, along with a vehicle for his use.

What Foulkes said

In a statement reported by NBC 10, Foulkes called the package indefensible and said it points to a larger problem. She acknowledged progress at T.F. Green, including new nonstop routes, new airline partners, a terminal upgrade and stronger finances, but said that progress does not justify the pay. She said quasi-public agencies are created by state law, are governed by boards largely appointed by the governor and are entrusted with public assets, yet often lack transparency and accountability.

Foulkes said that once in office she will review executive pay across quasi-public agencies, appoint board members who answer to the public, and work with the General Assembly on changes requiring public disclosure and reasonable limits on executive compensation.

What the airport board said

The airport corporation said an independent study it commissioned supports the raise and reflects pay in the airport industry, according to NBC 10. The board said it weighed the cost against the corporation's finances and the value of keeping steady leadership during the renovation of the T.F. Green terminal. Board Chairman Jonathan Savage praised Ahmad's dedication and record of growth in a statement, and the board passed a resolution crediting him with a financial turnaround and an expanded network of flights. The corporation also said most peer airports offer their chief executives a traditional pension, which it does not.

The board's resolution noted that T.F. Green was named the top domestic airport in Travel + Leisure's readers' survey in 2025 and 2026.

How it compares

NBC 10 reported that the industry study the airport released shows no leader of a small airport, the category in which the corporation places T.F. Green, earning more than about $920,000 a year, and that only two leaders of medium-sized airports earn just over $1 million. The chief executive of Massport, which runs Boston's Logan Airport, recently received a raise to $481,000 plus a 10% bonus, the station reported.

What it means and what comes next

For travelers, the contract does not change fares or fees directly, since the pay is drawn from airport revenue. The larger question is how the state's quasi-public agencies, which manage public assets and set their own executive pay, will be overseen under the new administration. Foulkes takes office Jan. 5. NBC 10's report did not include a response from the outgoing McKee administration, and no timeline for the review or any legislation has been announced.
