Foulkes Calls Airport CEO's New Pay Deal 'Indefensible,' Seeks Pause on Raises

The governor-elect asked Rhode Island's quasi-public agencies to hold off on executive contracts after the airport board approved a package worth more than $2 million a year.

Governor-elect Helena Buonanno Foulkes on Monday asked Rhode Island's quasi-public agencies to stop approving new or extended contracts and raises for their top executives until she takes office in January, after the board that runs T.F. Green International Airport approved a three-year deal for its chief executive worth more than $2 million a year. Foulkes called the package for Rhode Island Airport Corporation President and CEO Iftikhar Ahmad "indefensible," according to NBC 10 and the Rhode Island Current.

What happened

The Rhode Island Airport Corporation board signed the new contract with Ahmad on Nov. 12, the Rhode Island Current reported. The agreement is retroactive to Oct. 13, 2026, the day his previous contract expired. NBC 10, which first reported the deal on Friday, Nov. 13, described it as worth about $2.2 million a year. The Rhode Island Current put the maximum pay and benefits for the 2027 fiscal year at $2.1 million.

Ahmad has led the airport corporation for about a decade. The agency oversees T.F. Green in Warwick and five other state airports.

The numbers

According to the Rhode Island Current, the package includes a base salary of nearly $688,000, nearly $3.3 million in retention payments spread over six installments across three years, retirement benefits and a performance bonus of up to 20 percent. NBC 10 reported that the contract also provides 12 weeks a year of vacation, sick and personal time, part of which can be cashed out if unused. The Current reported the deal includes a car with all costs covered.

Both outlets reported that Ahmad's pay comes from airport revenues, not state taxpayer dollars.

What each side said

The board's resolution pointed to the airport's financial turnaround, credit rating upgrades, and growth in service, passengers and facilities, the Rhode Island Current reported. Board Chair Jonathan N. Savage praised Ahmad's record of growth, and the board said keeping him was important for continuity during major construction and expansion projects. The board hired the consulting firm Korn Ferry, which studied pay for 25 comparable airport executives. The study placed Ahmad's retention package around the 95th percentile of similar awards.

Foulkes said in her statement that the package is more than double what the same board approved a year ago and was approved only weeks before a new administration takes office, according to NBC 10. She also credited the airport with recent progress, including new nonstop routes, terminal upgrades and stronger finances. She said the agencies exist to serve the public and should be held to that standard.

Carlos Aramayo, president of UNITE Here Local 26, which represents concession workers at the airport, backed Foulkes, the Current reported. He contrasted the raise with base hourly wages for food and beverage workers, who he said have gone more than 15 months without a contract.

An airport corporation spokesperson did not immediately respond to requests for comment, the Current reported.

What it means for residents

Rhode Island has 15 quasi-public agencies, which make their own financial and leadership decisions but are created by state law. The airport corporation's seven unpaid board members are appointed by the governor and confirmed by the state Senate. Savage, appointed in 2014, has continued serving after his term expired in June 2025, which the Current noted is common when no replacement is named. That means the next governor will have a chance to reshape the board through appointments.

What comes next

Foulkes takes office on Jan. 5, 2027. She said she will then order a full review of executive pay across all quasi-public agencies, appoint board members who answer to Rhode Islanders, and work with the General Assembly on rules for transparency, disclosure and reasonable limits on executive pay. Neither outlet reported a timeline for the review.
