Stargazing Nights at Frosty Drew Observatory in Charlestown

Next dated session: Friday, Nov. 13, 7 to 9 p.m., weather permitting, with $5 admission per person.

On clear Friday evenings, the Frosty Drew Observatory and Science Center in Charlestown opens for Stargazing Nights, a chance to look up through real telescopes with astronomers on hand. The organizer lists sessions by date. The next one is Friday, Nov. 13, from 7 to 9 p.m., weather permitting, and it can run later depending on the sky.

Visitors can spend time in the observatory itself, in the Sky Theatre, and at the telescopes. The astronomers aim the instruments at the night's targets, which the organizers list as Saturn's rings, the craters of the Moon, Jupiter's four large moons, the polar ice caps of Mars, the phases of Venus, bright and massive stars, star clusters, nebulae and distant galaxies. The observatory also promotes what it calls the best Milky Way views in Rhode Island, so a clear, dark evening is the best time to come.

Admission is $5 per person. The organizer’s page gives the age cutoff inconsistently, so confirm it before you go. The observatory sits at 62 Park Lane in Ninigret Park, Charlestown, RI 02813. Because sessions are listed by date, a family can check the schedule and pick a clear night that fits its calendar, and a red-light flashlight helps keep night vision sharp once the lights go down.

For questions or the current schedule, call (401) 859-1450, email astronomer@frostydrew.org, or visit frostydrew.org/events.
