Helena Foulkes Wins Rhode Island Governor's Race in a Three-Way Contest

The Democrat and former CVS executive took about 57.5 percent of the vote over independent Ken Block and Republican Aaron Guckian.

Democrat Helena Foulkes has been elected Rhode Island's next governor, winning a three-way race on Tuesday, Nov. 3, with about 57.5 percent of the vote, according to results reported by The Boston Globe. The Associated Press called the race at about 8:35 p.m. on election night, and both of her main opponents conceded the same evening.

How the vote broke down

Independent Ken Block, a software company president and the founder of the Moderate Party of Rhode Island, finished second with about 27.9 percent, according to the Globe. Republican Aaron Guckian finished third with about 13.3 percent. Two other independent candidates, Jasjit S. Gotra and C.D. Reynolds, were also on the ballot.

The general election followed a September Democratic primary in which Foulkes defeated Gov. Dan McKee by 62.5 percent to 37.5 percent, carrying all 39 cities and towns, the Globe reported. The paper noted that McKee was the first sitting Rhode Island governor in 32 years to lose a primary.

Money in the race

Campaign finance figures cited by the Globe show a wide spending gap. Foulkes spent about $6.77 million from January 2025 through Oct. 26, compared with about $382,000 for Block and about $139,000 for Guckian.

Who the governor-elect is

Foulkes spent 25 years at CVS Health, including as president of CVS Pharmacy, and later served as chief executive of Hudson's Bay Company from 2018 to 2020, according to the Globe. She has served on the board of Costco since 2023. She has said she will step down from her corporate board seats, including Costco and Bank of America, before she is sworn in.

What each candidate said

In her victory speech in Providence, Foulkes told supporters that voters had made a choice about what kind of state Rhode Island wants to be, the Globe reported. NBC 10 WJAR reported that she spoke with reporters and thanked supporters at a Providence restaurant on Wednesday, describing a desire for change among voters.

Block called Foulkes to congratulate her and said the state needs her to succeed, according to the Globe. He described his own campaign as one built on ideas and solutions without a party label, and thanked his supporters. Guckian also conceded, called Foulkes to offer his support and said it was time for everyone to come together.

What it means for residents

Foulkes campaigned on a set of priorities that touch daily life across the state. According to the Globe, she has pledged to build 20,000 new homes and apartments, attract new companies to Rhode Island and finish rebuilding the Washington Bridge, whose closure has snarled traffic between Providence and the East Bay. She has also said she wants to reform the system that has left many of the state's bridges and roads in poor condition, and that bridge conditions should be made transparent to residents.

She said she will run a national search for a new director of the state Department of Transportation, seeking someone with no ties to Rhode Island who can start with a clean slate, the Globe reported. She has also named housing and education as shared priorities with Providence's incoming mayor, David Morales.

What comes next

Foulkes now begins her transition into the governor's office, with McKee serving out the rest of his term. Residents can expect early attention on her choice to lead the Department of Transportation and on the Washington Bridge rebuild, along with the state budget she will need to present to the General Assembly next year.
