CVS Will Split Into Two Companies. Here's What It Could Mean for Rhode Island

The Woonsocket-based company plans to separate its pharmacies from Aetna and Caremark, raising questions about jobs and headquarters.

CVS Health, the Woonsocket-based company that is among Rhode Island's largest and best-known employers, has announced it will split into two separate companies, according to Ocean State Media. The move would separate the company's familiar neighborhood pharmacies from its insurance and pharmacy benefit businesses, and it is prompting questions about what the change could mean for jobs, headquarters space and the local economy in the Ocean State.

What happened

Under the plan described by Ocean State Media, one of the new companies would hold CVS's roughly 9,000 retail pharmacies along with its MinuteClinic locations. The second company would hold Aetna, the health insurer CVS acquired, and Caremark, its pharmacy benefit manager. Ocean State Media described the decision as a major strategic reversal, because CVS spent roughly two decades assembling a single, deeply integrated parent company. The outlet's report did not include a timeline for completing the separation.

What it could mean for Woonsocket

Liam Rice, an analyst at Bryant University who spoke with Ocean State Media, said there has been no word yet on whether any headquarters operations would move, but he called it something worth watching. In the short term, he said, having two corporate headquarters in Woonsocket would be a benefit to the city. Over time, he said, he expects the pharmacy company to remain in Woonsocket, while the Aetna and Caremark company does not have the same strong tie to Rhode Island and could end up based somewhere else.

Rice said a full relocation out of the state is possible but unlikely. He suggested that Pawtucket, which has recently vacated space available, could make a pitch to host operations, and he encouraged that city's leaders to pursue the company.

Ocean State Media noted that CVS has made layoffs at its Woonsocket headquarters since 2024. The report did not give a current total for the number of CVS employees in Rhode Island.

Why the company is splitting

Rice told the outlet that CVS has not found enough savings from owning pharmacies and benefit businesses under one roof to justify the costs of the combined structure. He also pointed to bills in state legislatures aimed at pharmacy benefit managers and pharmacies, saying they suggest a fully integrated model may face growing limits.

On the company's finances, Rice said CVS went through a rough stretch, mainly on the insurance side and especially in Medicare Advantage plans, a problem he said was common across the industry. He described the past year as a solid turnaround, with the insurance business recovering, pharmacies growing and consolidation helping profits. He said he sees no near-term threat of local CVS store closures tied to the split.

What it means for residents

For Rhode Islanders who fill prescriptions at a neighborhood CVS, Rice said the split is not expected to have an immediate effect on what they pay for medicine. He said it could, however, be an early step toward broader regulation of insurance and pharmacy companies, a subject he connected to the affordability concerns voters raised in this month's elections. Ocean State Media also reported that health insurance premiums are expected to rise an average of 13 percent next year, with open enrollment now underway.

Rice said the change could also help the state's independent and small-chain pharmacies, which he said have long been overshadowed by CVS and Caremark. A separation could improve their bargaining position and pricing and bring more competition, he said, though he cautioned that their position is not yet secure.

What comes next

Ocean State Media noted that Rhode Island's incoming governor previously worked at CVS and has said publicly that she expects CVS Pharmacy to remain in the state, though Rice pointed out that the decision rests with the company, not state government. Key details still to come include the timetable for the split, where each new company will base its leadership, and whether any Rhode Island jobs shift as a result.
