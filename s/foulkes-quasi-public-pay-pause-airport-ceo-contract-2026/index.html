Foulkes Asks Quasi-Public Boards to Pause Executive Pay Deals Until January

The governor-elect called a new airport chief's pay package indefensible and promised a review of executive pay across Rhode Island's quasi-public agencies.

Gov.-elect Helena Buonanno Foulkes has asked Rhode Island's quasi-public agencies to hold off on new or extended executive contracts and pay raises until she takes office in January, after the board that runs T.F. Green International Airport approved a new three-year contract for its chief executive worth more than $2 million a year, according to Rhode Island Current and NBC 10.

What happened

The Rhode Island Airport Corporation board signed a new contract with President and CEO Iftikhar Ahmad on Nov. 12, Rhode Island Current reported. NBC 10's I-Team first reported the deal on Friday, Nov. 13. The contract is retroactive to Oct. 13, 2026, when Ahmad's previous agreement expired. Ahmad was hired in 2016 and oversees T.F. Green and the state's five other airports.

In a statement on Monday, Nov. 16, Foulkes called the package indefensible and said it points to a larger problem. She asked quasi-public boards to wait on executive compensation decisions and contract extensions until the new administration is in place, according to both outlets.

The numbers

Rhode Island Current reported that the contract allows up to $2.1 million in pay and benefits for fiscal 2027, more than double the roughly $900,000 Ahmad received in fiscal 2026. The package includes a base salary of nearly $688,000, a 20% performance bonus, retirement benefits and nearly $3.3 million in retention payments spread over six installments across three years. NBC 10 put the annual total at about $2.2 million. Both outlets reported that the pay comes from airport revenue rather than state tax dollars.

A consultant hired by the board, Korn Ferry, studied 25 comparable airport executives and projected 2026 pay and benefits ranging from about $472,000 to $1.2 million, Rhode Island Current reported. The firm's presentation placed Ahmad's retention package in the 95th percentile of comparable awards and said T.F. Green, though classified as a small hub, operates closer to the scope of a medium hub.

What each side said

The airport board's resolution credited Ahmad with a financial turnaround, improved credit ratings, expanded air service, passenger growth and infrastructure work at the Warwick airport. Board chair Jonathan N. Savage praised Ahmad's decade of work in a Nov. 12 statement, and the board said the pay was needed to keep leadership steady during major capital projects, according to Rhode Island Current. An airport spokesperson did not immediately respond to the outlet's request for comment.

Foulkes said the contract was approved weeks before a change in administration and is more than double what the same board approved a year earlier, NBC 10 reported. She also acknowledged progress at T.F. Green, including new nonstop routes, new airline partners and a terminal upgrade, but said that progress does not justify the decision. She said quasi-public agencies operate with too little transparency and accountability.

The president of UNITE Here Local 26, which represents concession workers at the airport, backed Foulkes's position and noted that food and beverage workers there have gone more than 15 months without a contract, according to Rhode Island Current.

What it means for residents

Rhode Island has 15 quasi-public agencies, including Rhode Island Commerce, the Rhode Island Public Transit Authority, the Convention Center Authority and the Quonset Development Corporation. They make their own financial and leadership decisions, but their board members are appointed by the governor and confirmed by the state Senate. The airport corporation's seven board members are unpaid.

What comes next

Foulkes takes office Jan. 5. She has said that once in office she will order a full review of executive pay across quasi-public agencies, appoint board members who see themselves as accountable to Rhode Islanders, and work with the General Assembly on transparency, disclosure and pay limits, according to NBC 10. Her transition team did not immediately say whether it would take a position on other recent raises at the agencies, Rhode Island Current reported.
