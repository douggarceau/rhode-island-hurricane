Rhode Island Votes Today: An Open Race for Governor and $600 Million in Bonds

With Gov. Dan McKee out after the primary, five candidates are on the ballot for governor, and five statewide bond questions total $600 million.

Rhode Islanders pick a new governor today. Gov. Dan McKee lost the Sept. 8 Democratic primary to Helena Foulkes, so whoever wins, the state will have a new leader in January.

The race for governor

Five candidates are on the general election ballot: Democrat Helena Foulkes, Republican Aaron Guckian, and independents Ken Block, Jasjit Gotra and Christopher Reynolds. Foulkes won the Democratic primary over McKee, and Guckian edged Elaine Pelino in a close Republican primary.

Five statewide questions, $600 million

Every ballot in the state carries the same five bond questions. Together they would authorize $600 million in borrowing.

Question 1, higher education, $275 million: a new Integrated Health Building at the University of Rhode Island in Kingston ($165 million), a renovated Adams Library and student success center at Rhode Island College ($50 million), and a Workforce Innovation Center at CCRI's Knight Campus in Warwick ($60 million).

Question 2, housing, $120 million: money for housing production, including at least $25 million aimed at homeownership and up to $10 million for a public housing development program.

Question 3, economic development, $100 million: $55 million for site work such as land, cleanup and utilities, including at Quonset Business Park and Providence's I-195 District, and $45 million for infrastructure serving ocean, defense, life sciences and data and cybersecurity industries.

Question 4, arts and culture, $50 million: $45 million to build a State History Center on Smith Hill in Providence and $5 million for state preservation grants.

Question 5, green economy, $55 million: flood and climate resilience ($25 million), state parks, beaches and campgrounds ($12 million), Narragansett Bay and watershed protection ($7 million), plus smaller amounts for brownfield cleanup, farmland, open space, local recreation and marine infrastructure.

Bonds are repaid with interest over time, so the total cost to taxpayers is higher than the amounts on the ballot.

Local questions too

Many towns have their own questions on the ballot. In Jamestown, for example, voters decide on a $3 million affordable housing bond and choose a new Town Council.

Before you go

Look up your polling place and your exact ballot at the Secretary of State's Voter Information Center, vote.sos.ri.gov. Official results will be posted tonight at electionresults.ri.gov, and we'll report them when they're in.
