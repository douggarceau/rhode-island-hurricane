Turn It Up, Chekhov: Indie-Rock Musical 'Seagulls' Lands at the Wilbury

Beth Hyland's college-band take on "The Seagull" is playing the Wilbury Theatre Group in Providence through December 13.

Chekhov wrote about a young actress, a restless young writer and the older stars who wreck them. Beth Hyland took that skeleton, plugged it into an amp and turned it into a college band story. Her indie-rock musical "Seagulls" is now playing at the Wilbury Theatre Group in Providence's Valley Arts District, where it runs through December 13, according to the Visit Rhode Island event listing.

What it is

The Visit Rhode Island listing bills the Wilbury run as the regional premiere of a new musical that reworks Chekhov's most heartbreaking play as a story about four college musicians, two love triangles and one industry predator, with many of the songs performed live by the actors. The listing frames the show as being about ambition, desire and the damage done to young women in a world that was not built to protect them.

Critics who saw an earlier production by Oak Park Festival Theatre outside Chicago in 2023 traced it to "The Seagull." Third Coast Review's Susan Lieberman wrote that the show draws inspiration from Chekhov's play but charts its own course, and described it as an indie-rock musical running about 90 minutes with no intermission.

The band and the story

According to the 2023 reviews, the band at the center of the show is called Seagulls. Con leads it while living in the shadow of his mother, a faded rock star. His girlfriend Nina is a singer and composer, and Masha, who has feelings for Con, is dating Simon. Talkin' Broadway's review notes that Con's mother, a former pop star, arrives with her new boyfriend, an indie-rock icon who takes an interest in Nina. That is the Chekhov engine, swapped from a country estate to the music business.

The songs are part of the world of the play. Talkin' Broadway wrote that every number except one is performed within the story, singling out "Nina's Song" as a devastating confessional and calling "Disaster Song," a No Wave-inspired number, the highlight of the first act.

Who is behind it

Hyland wrote the show, according to the Visit Rhode Island listing, which credits music direction to Carrie Tully. The Providence cast listed there is Charlotte Gray, Michael Eckenreiter, Jessie March and Carlos Fruzetti. The Wilbury Theatre Group, which presents it, is also the company behind the FringePVD festival.

What the critics said

The 2023 notices were mixed but agreed on one thing: the cast's energy. Talkin' Broadway said that production "crackles with an undeniable energy," while also arguing that the show never quite settles whose story it is telling and packs too many themes into 90 minutes. Third Coast Review praised the performances and the vocal power while finding some of the drama underdeveloped, and called Nina's arc the most convincing thread. The Visit Rhode Island listing quotes Chicago OnStage on the same "undeniable energy." The Providence production is a new staging with a new cast, so those reviews speak to the material, not to this run.

Why it matters

A rock show that lives inside a theater is a different night out from a club gig, and "Seagulls" sits right where the Hurricane likes to hang out: young musicians, a band on the edge of breaking, and the people in the business who circle around talent. It is also a chance to see a new musical early in its life, before it has been polished by a long run of regional stagings.

How to go

The Visit Rhode Island listing places the show at the Wilbury Theatre Group, 475 Valley Street in Providence, with performances on Thursdays, Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays at 7 p.m. through December 13. It lists tickets at $10 to $55 and notes Wilbury's pay-what-you-can "Access for All" program. Schedules can shift, so check thewilburygroup.org or call (401) 400-7100 before you head out. The story deals with exploitation in the music industry, so it plays as a show for grown-up audiences.
