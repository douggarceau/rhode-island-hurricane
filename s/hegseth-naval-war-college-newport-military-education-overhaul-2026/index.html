Hegseth Announces Military Education Overhaul at Newport's Naval War College

In a Nov. 9 visit, the Secretary of War issued 33 directives, including an end to tenure for newly hired civilian faculty at senior service colleges.

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth used a visit to the U.S. Naval War College in Newport on Monday to announce a sweeping overhaul of how the nation's senior military colleges teach officers, including an end to tenure for newly hired civilian professors, according to reports by What's Up Newp and the Rhode Island Current.

What happened

Hegseth spoke for about 30 minutes to roughly 700 students, faculty and staff in the college's Spruance Hall on Nov. 9 and did not take questions, the Rhode Island Current reported. The same day, the department issued an eight-page memorandum containing 33 directives that apply to six senior service colleges, according to the reports.

What's Up Newp reported that Hegseth told the audience the colleges exist to teach victory in war, and that he said their primary task "must be teaching victory." The Rhode Island Current reported that he described some academic traditions at the schools as relics of 19th century academia.

The numbers and the changes

According to the reports, the directives include several major changes:

- Newly hired civilian faculty would no longer be eligible for tenure or emeritus status. They would instead receive renewable three-year contracts judged on teaching and national security research.

- Each senior service college would add "warfighter chair" positions, filled by colonels and lieutenant colonels, or their Navy equivalents, coming from recent operational commands.

- Standardized end-of-course wargames and comprehensive exams would replace what the department called subjective seminar grading.

- The Under Secretary of War for Personnel and Readiness would oversee and track each requirement.

The changes follow a curriculum review of the military schools that Hegseth ordered in March, What's Up Newp reported. Under Secretary Anthony Tata led that review and traveled to Newport with Hegseth. The Rhode Island Current noted that Hegseth ended the college's partnership with Brown University last month, a relationship dating to 2014.

Who said what

Rear Adm. Darryl Walker, the college's president, called the visit an extraordinary opportunity for the college community and said Hegseth's direction would continue to guide its programs, according to What's Up Newp.

A college spokesperson, Lt. Cmdr. Seth Koenig, told the Rhode Island Current that the president and provost will review the directives against the current curriculum, and said some of the items are not in direct conflict with what the college already does.

The Rhode Island Current reported that about 30 to 40 protesters gathered near the Pell Bridge off-ramp during the visit. Neither report included comment from Rhode Island's congressional delegation, and a Brown University spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment, the Current reported.

Separately, Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, announced the same day that he is forcing a House vote on articles of impeachment against Hegseth, alleging violations of the War Powers Resolution, according to both outlets. The department had not responded publicly to that effort, What's Up Newp reported.

What it means for Newport

The Naval War College, founded in Newport in 1884, is one of Aquidneck Island's best-known institutions and a major employer. About 70% of its faculty are civilians, according to the Rhode Island Current, so the end of tenure for new hires would shape how the college recruits professors in the years ahead. The reports did not say whether current faculty contracts would change.

What comes next

The 33 requirements will be carried out under the oversight of the Under Secretary for Personnel and Readiness, though the reports did not list deadlines. The college's leadership is reviewing the directives, and the Rhode Island Current reported that Hegseth planned to hold a press conference on Tuesday. No date has been set for the House vote Massie is seeking.
